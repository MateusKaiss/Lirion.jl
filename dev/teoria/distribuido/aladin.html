ALADIN

ALADIN, the Augmented Lagrangian Alternating Direction Inexact Newton method of [2], is the second distributed solver in Lirion. Like ADMM it lets each region solve its own OPF and reconciles the boundary copies afterwards, but the reconciliation is different in kind. ADMM averages the copies and raises a price, asking each region for nothing but its solution. ALADIN asks each region for its local sensitivities, the gradient of its cost, an approximation of the Hessian of its Lagrangian, and the Jacobian of its active constraints, and a central coordinator solves one equality-constrained QP that moves all the regions at once. The step is Newton-like, so close to the solution it takes far fewer iterations than ADMM. The trade is information: the coordinator must see curvature and active sets, not just boundary values, and a model that supplies only part of that curvature, the conic SOCWR, is coordinated poorly, its step stalling near the conic solver's accuracy. This page derives the iteration; the knobs, rho, mu, the globalizations and the two stopping criteria, are on the ALADIN settings page.

What the ALADIN certificate guarantees

ALADIN stops only when two residuals are small at the same time: the consensus mismatch between the regional copies, and the stationarity residual $\rho\lVert\Sigma(y-x)\rVert_1$, the size of the proximal step. [2] shows that the pair implies the local solutions satisfy the first-order KKT conditions of the full problem up to the tolerance, with the coordinator multiplier $\lambda$ as the dual of the coupling. The stop therefore certifies an approximate KKT point, not merely agreement at the boundary. The ADMM stop also tests two residuals, but its second one is the motion of the consensus, $\rho\lVert z^{k+1} - z^{k}\rVert$, a proxy for dual feasibility; it never evaluates stationarity at the regional solutions.

Derivation

The structured problem

Partition the network into regions $r = 1, \dots, R$ as on the sectioning page, and let $x^{(r)}$ collect the variables region $r$ owns, its ghost copies included. Each region has a cost $f_r$ and a feasible set $\mathcal{X}_r$ (its own balance, flow, bound, and angle constraints). The consistency constraints couple the regions through their copies, and nothing else does. Written as one problem,

\[\min_{x^{(1)},\dots,x^{(R)}} \;\sum_{r=1}^{R} f_r\big(x^{(r)}\big) \quad\text{s.t.}\quad x^{(r)} \in \mathcal{X}_r, \qquad \sum_{r=1}^{R} A_r\, x^{(r)} = 0 .\]

The matrix $A_r$ picks out, with a sign, the boundary quantities region $r$ holds a copy of. For a tie-line cut with one shared angle $\theta_h$ between two regions, $A_1 x^{(1)} = \theta_h^{(1)}$ and $A_2 x^{(2)} = -\theta_h^{(2)}$, so the coupling row reads $\theta_h^{(1)} - \theta_h^{(2)} = 0$. Under :power the row is a difference of flows, which in the DC model are themselves linear in the angles; under :split_bus it is a sum of injections. In DC, and under state coupling for every model, these rows are affine, the form [2] assumes. Under AC power or hybrid coupling the shared flow is a nonlinear function of the region's variables, which Lirion linearizes in the coordinator QP. That is an extension beyond the paper's affine assumption, with the same fixed points. Every coupling row carries one multiplier $\lambda_j$, the price of its mismatch, and $\lambda$ is shared by all the regions that enter the row.

Step 1: the local proximal subproblem

Each region cannot see the others, so it is given two things to stand in for them: the current price vector $\lambda$ and its own previous iterate $x^{(r)}$, which acts as a proximal centre. The region then solves

\[y^{(r)} \in \arg\min_{y \in \mathcal{X}_r}\; f_r(y) + \lambda^{\top} A_r\, y + \frac{\rho}{2}\,\lVert y - x^{(r)} \rVert_{\Sigma_r}^{2} .\]

The linear term charges the region the price of its boundary copies. The quadratic term, with $\lVert v \rVert_{\Sigma}^2 = v^{\top}\Sigma v$, pulls the solution toward the last agreed point, with the penalty $\rho$ (rho, default $10^3$) setting how hard. The weight matrix $\Sigma_r$ is diagonal and groups the variables into classes: on DC the shared boundary angles carry a weight of 10 and the generator setpoints and everything else a weight of 1, so the proximal term holds the boundary closer than the interior. The subproblem keeps all of the region's own constraints, so $y^{(r)}$ is always locally feasible; what it lacks is agreement with the neighbours.

Why a proximal term and not an ADMM penalty

ADMM penalizes the distance to the consensus value. ALADIN penalizes the distance to the region's own previous point and leaves the consensus to the coordinator. The two coincide for a particular choice of the matrices ($\Sigma_r = A_r^{\top}A_r$, see section 5 of [2]), which is how the paper shows that one ALADIN iteration costs the same as one ADMM iteration when the coordinator is given the ADMM-equivalent curvature. The gain comes from giving it better curvature.

On the first iteration the proximal centre is the solver's start values; afterwards Lirion resets it to the first local solution, $x^{(r)} \leftarrow y^{(r)}$, so the proximal step is zero at $k = 1$ and convergence is only certified from the second iteration on.

Step 2: the sensitivities each region exports

After its solve, region $r$ reports three objects about the point $y^{(r)}$.

\[g_r = \nabla f_r\big(y^{(r)}\big), \qquad B_r \approx \nabla^2\Big[f_r + \kappa_r^{\top} h_r\Big]\big(y^{(r)}\big), \qquad C_r = \frac{\partial h_r^{\mathrm{act}}}{\partial y}\Big|_{y^{(r)}} .\]

The vector $g_r$ is the cost gradient. The matrix $B_r$ is a symmetric, positive-semidefinite approximation of the Hessian of the region's Lagrangian, with $\kappa_r$ the local multipliers of the constraints $h_r \le 0$. The matrix $C_r$ is the Jacobian of the constraints that are active at $y^{(r)}$: every equality row, every inequality whose multiplier is non-zero beyond a tolerance, and every variable sitting on a bound (within $10^{-6}$ of it). Linearly dependent rows are dropped so the active Jacobian has full row rank. This is the inexact-Newton half of the name: $B_r$ and $C_r$ need only approximate the true matrices for the method to converge, and how well they do sets the local rate.

What $B_r$ contains depends on the model. On DC the cost is the only curvature, so $B_r$ is the diagonal of quadratic cost coefficients on the generator columns with a small floor elsewhere. On AC and on SOCWRNL the region is a smooth NLP, and Lirion assembles the exact Lagrangian Hessian $\nabla^2 f_r + \sum_j \kappa_j \nabla^2 h_j$ from the solver's evaluator, adds the proximal curvature $\rho\Sigma_r$, and shifts the diagonal by the smallest $\delta I$ that makes the result positive semidefinite. On the conic SOCWR the cones have no smooth Hessian to read, so $B_r$ falls back to the DC-style diagonal plus $\rho\Sigma_r$, and the cones contribute only their active-face normals to $C_r$. That missing curvature is the limitation described on the sectioning page, and the reason SOCWRNL exists.

Step 3: the coordinator QP

The coordinator now has, for every region, a locally feasible point and a quadratic model of the region's behaviour around it. It looks for the correction $\Delta y^{(r)}$ of each point that closes the coupling while keeping the summed quadratic models as small as possible, which is a QP,

\[\begin{aligned} \min_{\Delta y,\, s}\quad & \sum_{r=1}^{R} \Big[\tfrac12\, \Delta y^{(r)\top} B_r\, \Delta y^{(r)} + g_r^{\top} \Delta y^{(r)}\Big] + \lambda^{\top} s + \tfrac{\mu}{2}\, \lVert s \rVert_2^2 \\ \text{s.t.}\quad & \sum_{r=1}^{R} A_r \big(y^{(r)} + \Delta y^{(r)}\big) = s \quad\big|\ \lambda_{\mathrm{QP}}, \\ & C_r\, \Delta y^{(r)} = 0, \qquad r = 1, \dots, R . \end{aligned}\]

The first constraint is the coupling, written on the corrected points and relaxed by a slack $s$: the coordinator may leave a residual mismatch, but pays $\lambda^{\top}s + \tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert s\rVert^2$ for it. The penalty $\mu$ (mu, default $10^4$) is the coordinator's own augmented-Lagrangian weight, separate from the regions' $\rho$; as $\mu \to \infty$ the slack closes and the row becomes a hard equality. The second constraint keeps each correction inside the tangent space of the region's active set, so the QP does not propose moving a generator past a bound that the local solve already found binding; active bounds enter as $\Delta y_k = 0$. The multiplier $\lambda_{\mathrm{QP}}$ of the coupling rows is the coordinator's new estimate of the price. In the two-region tie-line example the whole coupling block is the single row $\theta_h^{(1)} + \Delta\theta_h^{(1)} - \theta_h^{(2)} - \Delta\theta_h^{(2)} = s$, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{QP}}$ is one number.

The QP is convex when every $B_r$ is positive semidefinite, which the shift $\delta I$ guarantees, and it is structured: a block-diagonal Hessian tied only by the coupling rows, with the corrections and the slack as unknowns. Lirion hands it to an interior-point solver (Clarabel by default, qp_optimizer to change it), because the QP becomes badly conditioned on large networks and a first-order QP solver stops reaching tolerance there.

Why the slack

Without $s$ the coupling would be a hard equality and $\lambda_{\mathrm{QP}}$ its exact multiplier; the paper's section 4 shows the two forms are the same QP, with $\lambda_{\mathrm{QP}} = \lambda + \mu\, s$ at the optimum. The slack version is better conditioned when $\mu$ is large, because the large entries land on the diagonal, and it is always feasible: $\Delta y = 0$, $s = \sum_r A_r y^{(r)}$ is a feasible point whatever the active sets.

Step 4: the primal and dual update

Each region now holds three points: its previous iterate $x^{(r)}$, its local solution $y^{(r)}$, and the coordinator's correction $\Delta y^{(r)}$. The update blends them, with a separate step length on each move,

\[x^{(r)} \leftarrow x^{(r)} + \alpha_1\big(y^{(r)} - x^{(r)}\big) + \alpha_2\, \Delta y^{(r)}, \qquad \lambda \leftarrow \lambda + \alpha_3\big(\lambda_{\mathrm{QP}} - \lambda\big).\]

With $\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = \alpha_3 = 1$ this is the plain update of Algorithm 2 in [2]: the new iterate is the corrected local solution $y^{(r)} + \Delta y^{(r)}$, and the price jumps to the coordinator's estimate. The new $x^{(r)}$ becomes the proximal centre of the next local solve, and the new $\lambda$ its price, so the loop closes.

Step 5: globalization

The full step is what gives the fast local rate, but far from the solution it can overshoot, and Newton-type methods need a safeguard against that. ALADIN's safeguard is the choice of $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$. Lirion's default, :armijo_l2, keeps $\alpha_1$ and $\alpha_3$ at their configured values and backtracks $\alpha_2$ on the coordinator's own augmented Lagrangian,

\[\phi(\alpha) = \sum_{r} f_r\big(y^{(r)} + \alpha\,\Delta y^{(r)}\big) + \lambda^{\top} c(\alpha) + \tfrac{\mu}{2}\, \lVert c(\alpha) \rVert_2^2 , \qquad c(\alpha) = \sum_r A_r\big(y^{(r)} + \alpha\,\Delta y^{(r)}\big),\]

halving $\alpha$ from 1 until $\phi(\alpha) \le \phi(0) + 10^{-4}\,\alpha\,\phi'(0)$. On DC and SOCWR a search that reaches the floor $10^{-3}$ returns it; on AC it instead falls back to Algorithm 3's dual-only step ($\alpha_1 = \alpha_2 = 0$, a golden-section search on $\alpha_3$). The merit is the one the QP was built to decrease, so the test is cheap and local. The paper's own strategy, Algorithm 3, is an exact $\ell_1$ penalty with three branches (full step, local step only, or a dual-only line search on $\alpha_3$) and carries the global convergence proof; Lirion offers it as :alg3 and :alg3_original, and the undamped :full_step for well-behaved cases. Which one to use, and what the step coefficients do under each, is on the ALADIN settings page.

Step 6: the stopping rule

The iteration stops when the local solutions agree at the boundary and the proximal step has died out. Both are measured on the fresh local solutions $y^{(r)}$, before the update,

\[r^{\mathrm{cons}} = \Big\lVert \sum_{r} A_r\, y^{(r)} \Big\rVert , \qquad r^{\mathrm{stat}} = \rho\, \Big\lVert \big(\Sigma_r\,(y^{(r)} - x^{(r)})\big)_{r=1}^{R} \Big\rVert_1 ,\]

and the run is certified when $r^{\mathrm{cons}} < \epsilon^{\mathrm{cons}}$ and $r^{\mathrm{stat}} < \epsilon^{\mathrm{stat}}$ on the same iteration. The first residual is the consensus mismatch. The second is the gradient of the proximal term at $y^{(r)}$, which by the subproblem's own optimality condition equals $\nabla f_r + A_r^{\top}\lambda + C_r^{*\top}\kappa_r$ up to sign, exact only for an exact local solve and otherwise up to the regional solver's own KKT tolerance: a small $r^{\mathrm{stat}}$ means the current price $\lambda$ already makes each region's solution stationary for the coupled problem. Dropping it would leave a method that stops as soon as the copies agree, whatever the price, and that is not ALADIN's stop; step 2 of Algorithm 2 in [2] tests both.

The stationarity bar is $\epsilon^{\mathrm{stat}} = n_{\mathrm{prox}}\,\epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}$, with $n_{\mathrm{prox}}$ the number of variables carrying a proximal weight, the $\ell_1$ analogue of Boyd's dimension-scaled bound. The consensus bar depends on stopping_criterion. Under the default :boyd, $r^{\mathrm{cons}}$ is the 2-norm and the bar is Boyd's primal test $\sqrt{n}\,\epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}} + \epsilon^{\mathrm{rel}} \max(\lVert y_I\rVert, \lVert y_J\rVert)$ [1], with $n$ the number of coupling rows and $y_I, y_J$ the two sides of those rows (the copies on one side of each cut and on the other); under :hfd the bar is a flat $\epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}$ as in the paper, measured in the max-norm where the paper uses the 1-norm. Both tolerances default to $10^{-4}$. The paper notes that the test is not scale invariant, and neither is Lirion's. A third quantity, the dual step $\mu\lVert\lambda^{+} - \lambda\rVert$, is recorded for diagnostics but is not part of the stop test.

ALADIN iteration

The constructor aladin() builds one local model per region, one coordinator QP, and the loop below. It runs on dc(), ac(), socwr(), and socwrnl().

Sets

  • $\mathcal{R} = \{1, \dots, R\}$: regions (the bus.zone values).
  • $\mathcal{K}$: coupling rows, one per shared quantity (a boundary-bus state per copy pair, a boundary flow, a split-bus injection group), as fixed by the coupling and sectioning choice.
  • $\mathcal{A}_r \subseteq \{1, \dots, n_r\}$: columns of region $r$ on an active bound at the current local solution.

Parameters

  • $A_r \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{K}| \times n_r}$: signed selection of region $r$'s boundary copies into the coupling rows.
  • $\Sigma_r \succ 0$: diagonal proximal weights by variable class (sigma_theta_shared, sigma_pg, sigma_other on DC; fixed per class on the other models).
  • $\rho > 0$: local proximal penalty (rho, default $10^3$).
  • $\mu > 0$: coordinator slack penalty (mu, default $10^4$).
  • $\epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}, \epsilon^{\mathrm{rel}}$: tolerances (eps_abs, eps_rel, both $10^{-4}$); $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$: step coefficients (all $1$).

Variables

  • $x^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_r}$: current iterate of region $r$, the proximal centre.
  • $y^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_r}$: local solution of region $r$.
  • $\Delta y^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_r}$: coordinator correction for region $r$.
  • $s \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{K}|}$: coupling slack.
  • $\lambda, \lambda_{\mathrm{QP}} \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{K}|}$: coupling multipliers, current and coordinator-proposed.

Local subproblem

\[y^{(r)} \in \arg\min_{y \in \mathcal{X}_r}\; f_r(y) + \lambda^{\top} A_r\, y + \frac{\rho}{2}\,(y - x^{(r)})^{\top} \Sigma_r\, (y - x^{(r)}), \qquad r \in \mathcal{R}. \tag{1}\]

Sensitivities

\[g_r = \nabla f_r\big(y^{(r)}\big), \qquad B_r \approx \nabla^2\big[f_r + \kappa_r^{\top} h_r\big]\big(y^{(r)}\big) \succeq 0, \qquad C_r = \nabla h_r^{\mathrm{act}}\big(y^{(r)}\big)^{\top}, \qquad r \in \mathcal{R}. \tag{2}\]

Coordinator QP

\[\min_{\Delta y,\, s}\; \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \Big[\tfrac12\, \Delta y^{(r)\top} B_r\, \Delta y^{(r)} + g_r^{\top} \Delta y^{(r)}\Big] + \lambda^{\top} s + \tfrac{\mu}{2}\, s^{\top} s \tag{3}\]

\[\sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}} A_r \big(y^{(r)} + \Delta y^{(r)}\big) = s \quad\big|\ \lambda_{\mathrm{QP}} \tag{4}\]

\[C_r\, \Delta y^{(r)} = 0, \qquad \Delta y^{(r)}_i = 0,\ i \in \mathcal{A}_r, \qquad r \in \mathcal{R}. \tag{5}\]

Step update

\[x^{(r)} \leftarrow x^{(r)} + \alpha_1\big(y^{(r)} - x^{(r)}\big) + \alpha_2\, \Delta y^{(r)}, \qquad r \in \mathcal{R}, \tag{6}\]

\[\lambda \leftarrow \lambda + \alpha_3\big(\lambda_{\mathrm{QP}} - \lambda\big). \tag{7}\]

Residuals and tolerances

\[r^{\mathrm{cons}} = \Big\lVert \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}} A_r\, y^{(r)} \Big\rVert_{2\ \text{or}\ \infty}, \qquad r^{\mathrm{stat}} = \rho \sum_{r \in \mathcal{R}} \big\lVert \Sigma_r\,(y^{(r)} - x^{(r)}) \big\rVert_1 . \tag{8}\]

\[\epsilon^{\mathrm{cons}} = \begin{cases} \sqrt{|\mathcal{K}|}\,\epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}} + \epsilon^{\mathrm{rel}} \max\big(\lVert y_I \rVert_2, \lVert y_J \rVert_2\big) & \texttt{:boyd} \\[2pt] \epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}} & \texttt{:hfd} \end{cases}, \qquad \epsilon^{\mathrm{stat}} = n_{\mathrm{prox}}\,\epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}. \tag{9}\]

Under :boyd the norm in (8) is the 2-norm; under :hfd it is the max-norm. $y_I$ and $y_J$ stack the copies on the two sides of the coupling rows, and $n_{\mathrm{prox}}$ is the number of columns with a non-zero entry in some $\Sigma_r$.

The ALADIN step

Starting from $\lambda = 0$ and the first local solve as the proximal centre, repeat for $k = 1, 2, \dots$ until max_iter (default 100):

  1. Every region solves (1) in parallel at the current $(x^{(r)}, \lambda)$ and exports (2).
  2. Solve the QP (3)–(5) for $\Delta y$, $s$, and $\lambda_{\mathrm{QP}}$.
  3. Compute (8) and (9). If $r^{\mathrm{cons}} < \epsilon^{\mathrm{cons}}$ and $r^{\mathrm{stat}} < \epsilon^{\mathrm{stat}}$, and $k \ge 2$, stop: $y$ is the solution and $\lambda$ its coupling dual.
  4. Choose $\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3$ by the configured globalization (default: Armijo backtracking on $\alpha_2$).
  5. Apply (6) and (7). With adaptive = true, grow $\rho$ and $\mu$ by their factors, capped at their upper bounds. Return to 1.

What it captures / what it loses

Philosophy

Captures: the curvature of each region and its active set. Because the coordinator sees $B_r$ and $C_r$, its step is a Newton step on the coupled KKT system. Houska's quadratic rate needs exact Hessians, full steps, and $\mu \to \infty$ at a regular KKT point; with the fixed approximations and the proximal term Lirion ships, the guaranteed rate is linear, in practice much faster than ADMM's. The stop certifies stationarity as well as agreement, so a converged run is an approximate KKT point of the whole problem.

Loses: locality of information. ADMM exchanges boundary values only; ALADIN exports a gradient, a Hessian approximation, and an active Jacobian from every region to one coordinator, and solves a central QP each iteration. It also needs that curvature to exist: a region whose model is conic has no Lagrangian Hessian to export, which is why the conic SOCWR converges under ADMM and only stalls under ALADIN. And a Newton-type step must be globalized, which adds a line search and its own tuning.

Lirion vs. the original

Lirion vs. the original

Lirion follows Algorithm 2 of [2] in structure, with these departures.

The matrix $B_r$. The paper prescribes $B_r \approx \nabla^2 [f_r + \kappa_r^{\top} h_r]$, with the proximal term living in the local subproblem only. By default Lirion adds the proximal curvature $\rho\Sigma_r$ to $B_r$ on AC, SOCWR, and SOCWRNL, plus a $10^{-6}$ Tikhonov floor and whatever $\delta I$ shift is needed for positive semidefiniteness; on DC $B_r$ is the cost diagonal over the same $10^{-6}$ floor, and because the DC constraints are linear this is the exact Lagrangian Hessian. Adding $\rho\Sigma_r$ damps the correction and slows the local rate at large $\rho$, and it is the choice the shipped campaigns were run with.

Nonlinear coupling. The paper assumes the coupling $\sum_r A_r y_r = b$ is affine. It is, for DC and for state coupling. Under AC power or hybrid coupling, and under dummy-bus, the coupled flow is a nonlinear function of the region's variables, so Lirion linearizes it in the coordinator QP, and the coordinator curvature carries no $\lambda^{\top}\nabla^2 c$ term for that coupling. This is a Newton-type extension with the same fixed points, outside the affine setting the paper proves its rate for.

The conic SOCWR. The paper assumes twice-differentiable $f_r$ and $h_r$. The cones of the conic SOCWR are not, so its $B_r$ carries no cone curvature and its $C_r$ only the active cone-face normals; the coordinator step then makes little progress and the run settles near the conic solver's accuracy. ALADIN rejects :dummy_bus on socwr() for this reason, and SOCWRNL, which writes the cones as smooth inequalities, is the supported route to the Jabr relaxation under ALADIN.

The gradient. The paper's step 3 uses the modified gradient $g_r = \nabla f_r + (C_r^{*} - C_r)^{\top}\kappa_r$, correcting for inequalities left out of the active set. Lirion uses the plain cost gradient by default; the correction is an opt-in on the smooth NLP models.

The stop. The paper uses one tolerance and the $\ell_1$ norm on both residuals. Lirion keeps the $\ell_1$ stationarity residual with a dimension-scaled bar $n_{\mathrm{prox}}\,\epsilon^{\mathrm{abs}}$, and measures the consensus residual either by Boyd's $\sqrt{n}$-scaled 2-norm test (:boyd, the default) or by a flat bar in the paper's style (:hfd), measured in the max-norm rather than the paper's 1-norm. Both options test consensus and stationarity.

Globalization. The paper's Algorithm 3 is an exact $\ell_1$ penalty with three branches. Lirion's default is an Armijo backtracking on $\alpha_2$ alone, over the coordinator's augmented Lagrangian; Algorithm 3 is available as :alg3 and, with the paper's Wolfe branch plus a gradient fallback when the QP dual direction is not an ascent direction and a penalty weight fixed for the run, :alg3_original.

Active set. Active bounds are detected by primal distance ($10^{-6}$) and active inequalities by the size of their local multiplier, then dependent rows are removed. The paper leaves the choice of $C_r \approx C_r^{*}$ open.

SDPWRM

The SDPWRM model is a convex relaxation written in W-space. The SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) and QC (Quadratic Convex) relaxations tighten the voltage-product blocks one branch at a time. SDPWRM gathers every block into a single Hermitian matrix $W = V\,V^{*}$ over all buses and imposes one condition on it, positive semidefiniteness $W \succeq 0$. That single condition already contains the whole SOCWR relaxation as its $2 \times 2$ slice and adds the loop couplings SOCWR misses, up to the full network, so SDPWRM is never looser than SOCWR. It minimizes the same generation cost as the exact AC Polar model, over the convex set of semidefinite $W$ instead of over genuine voltages.

A convex relaxation

SDPWRM is a relaxation, not an approximation: it enlarges the feasible set (it drops the non-convex $\operatorname{rank}(W) = 1$ requirement), so its optimum is a guaranteed lower bound on the true AC cost. A semidefinite $W$ that is not rank one corresponds to no genuine voltage vector, so the bound can be loose, but it always errs low, never high.

Derivation

1. The local tie does not close the cycles

In the SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) the tie between the voltage-product blocks is local, holding branch by branch:

\[W_r^2 + W_i^2 \;\le\; W_{ii}\,W_{jj}.\]

Each branch settles its own accounts without consulting its neighbours, and in a meshed network that lets through a lie no single branch can catch. Each branch carries an angle buried in its blocks, $\theta_{ij} = \operatorname{atan2}(W_i, W_r)$, and nothing ties these angles to one another. Take a triangle of three lines, each reporting a $30^\circ$ difference. Branch by branch every one is impeccable, sitting exactly on the SOCWR cone. Walk the loop, though, and the three differences add to $30^\circ + 30^\circ + 30^\circ = 90^\circ$, when genuine bus angles would have to bring them back to zero:

\[(\theta_1 - \theta_2) + (\theta_2 - \theta_3) + (\theta_3 - \theta_1) = 0.\]

Three honest-looking branches, one impossible triangle. SOCWR never catches it, because it never looks at more than one branch at a time. The worked example at the end of this page carries the same triangle through in full.

The QC (Quadratic Convex) relaxation attacked this with the exact angle coupling $td = \theta_i - \theta_j$, which brings the bus angles back so that they telescope, but the link between $td$ and the blocks runs through the (relaxed) envelopes, so it only tightens as far as the envelopes squeeze. SDPWRM attacks from another direction: instead of improving branch by branch, it imposes a single condition on the entire network.

2. The central idea: the matrix W

Until now the blocks were indexed by branch (wr, wi) and by bus (wii). SDPWRM organizes them into a matrix. Define the vector of all complex bus voltages,

\[V = (V_1, V_2, \ldots, V_n),\]

and the matrix $W$ as the outer product $V V^{*}$, column times row. An inner product compresses two vectors into a single number; the outer product does the reverse, expanding them into an $n \times n$ matrix:

\[W = V\,V^{*}, \qquad\text{that is}\qquad W_{ij} = V_i\,V_j^{*}.\]

That entry $V_i V_j^{*}$ is the object behind the blocks of the AC Rectangular (ACR) model, $V_i V_j^{*} = W_r + j\,W_i$ (the imaginary unit is the plain $j$ of the AC Polar page; bus indices are only subscripts, so the two never collide). Hence:

  • diagonal: $W_{ii} = |V_i|^2$, the squared voltage magnitudes of every bus (the bus block wii);
  • off-diagonal: $W_{ij} = W_r + j\,W_i$, the cross blocks (wr, wi) of the pair $ij$.

Every block of every branch and bus lives in a single object. The matrix has an entry for every pair of buses, whether or not a line joins them.

Swapping the two indices conjugates the entry: $W_{ji} = V_j V_i^{*}$ is the complex conjugate of $W_{ij} = V_i V_j^{*}$. A matrix whose mirror entries are conjugates like this equals its own conjugate transpose, and is called Hermitian, the complex counterpart of a symmetric matrix. Section 6 leans on exactly this structure when it rebuilds $W$ over the reals.

3. Rank and cycle consistency

The matrix lets us ask the one question SOCWR could not: do all these blocks come from a single common $V$? The tool that answers it is rank.

The rank of a matrix is the number of linearly independent columns, how many distinct directions the columns actually occupy. A generic $3 \times 3$ matrix has rank 3, three columns pointing in distinct directions. If one column is a combination of the others, the rank drops. The extreme case, rank 1, means every column points the same way: each is a multiple of a single vector.

That extreme is our question answered yes. If $W$ genuinely factors as $V V^{*}$, every column is a multiple of that one $V$, so $W$ has rank 1. A single generator exists, each bus then carries one angle, and the differences telescope around any loop on their own:

\[\operatorname{rank}(W) = 1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{one generator } V \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{every cycle closes}.\]

Rank 1 is the whole of genuineness. It locks the magnitudes and the angles together onto one $V$. Cycle consistency is only its angle half. Keep that split in view. The relaxation is about to drop rank 1, and the question becomes how much of the cycle structure survives without it (Section 5).

4. Keep the convex half

The exact requirement is that $W$ have the form $V V^{*}$ for some $V \neq 0$, which is equivalent to two conditions, $W$ positive semidefinite (meaning $x^{*} W x \ge 0$ for every vector $x$, written $W \succeq 0$) and rank one:

\[W = V\,V^{*}\ \text{ for some } V \neq 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad W \succeq 0 \ \text{ and }\ \operatorname{rank}(W) = 1.\]

In OPF the bus voltages are bounded away from zero, so $V \neq 0$ always holds.

Any physical $W$ passes the semidefinite test for free:

\[x^{*} W x = x^{*} V V^{*} x = (V^{*}x)^{*}(V^{*}x) = |V^{*}x|^2 \;\ge\; 0,\]

a squared magnitude. So $W \succeq 0$ is a necessary condition for $W$ to come from real voltages. Rank one, on the other hand, is non-convex. Imposing $\operatorname{rank}(W) = 1$ would bring back the full difficulty of the AC problem. SDPWRM keeps only the convex half of the pair:

\[W \succeq 0, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad x^{*} W x \ge 0 \ \text{ for every vector } x,\]

with $x^{*}$ the conjugate transpose (the vector laid down and its entries conjugated).

5. The ladder of principal minors

What does $W \succeq 0$ actually impose? A principal submatrix is the block left when you pick a set of indices and keep their rows and columns; its minor is that block's determinant. The criterion is that $W \succeq 0$ if and only if all principal minors are $\ge 0$. This unfolds the single constraint into a ladder (one rung per block size), and each rung is an old acquaintance.

Rung 1 ($1 \times 1$ blocks). The diagonal entries: $W_{ii} \ge 0$.

Rung 2 ($2 \times 2$ blocks), the whole SOCWR. The block on $\{i,j\}$ is

\[\begin{bmatrix} W_{ii} & W_r + j\,W_i \\ W_r - j\,W_i & W_{jj} \end{bmatrix},\]

and its determinant $\ge 0$ gives $W_{ii}W_{jj} - (W_r^2 + W_i^2) \ge 0$, that is $W_r^2 + W_i^2 \le W_{ii}W_{jj}$. The SOCWR tie is the $2 \times 2$ minor of the SDP. It was not replaced, it was absorbed, and it follows for free that the SDP is never looser than SOCWR.

Rung 3 ($3 \times 3$ blocks), the cycle constraint. Here is the model's novelty, read off at the SOCWR cone boundary. Take the triangle $\{1,2,3\}$ and set every magnitude to 1, so the minor speaks about the angles alone. Each branch then picks its own angle through a unit-modulus block, $W_{12} = e^{j\alpha}$, $W_{23} = e^{j\beta}$, $W_{31} = e^{j\gamma}$, with $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ free and not yet tied to one another. The Hermitian submatrix is

\[\begin{bmatrix} 1 & e^{j\alpha} & e^{-j\gamma} \\ e^{-j\alpha} & 1 & e^{j\beta} \\ e^{j\gamma} & e^{-j\beta} & 1 \end{bmatrix}.\]

Writing $\xi = \alpha + \beta + \gamma$, the determinant is

\[\det = e^{j\xi} + e^{-j\xi} - 2 = 2(\cos\xi - 1),\]

using Euler's identity $e^{j\xi} + e^{-j\xi} = 2\cos\xi$. Since $\cos\xi \le 1$ always, the determinant can never be positive, while PSD demands $\det \ge 0$. Squeezed between the formula's natural ceiling and the constraint's floor, it survives only at $\det = 0$, which forces $\cos\xi = 1$, that is $\xi = 0$ once the per-branch apertures stay inside $\pm 90^\circ$. So on this boundary slice rung 3 is the cycle constraint, $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 0$, and only now does a common generator exist,

\[V = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ e^{-j\alpha} \\ e^{-j(\alpha+\beta)} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \gamma = -(\alpha + \beta),\]

with the submatrix equal to $V V^{*}$ and therefore rank one.

And the ladder continues. $4 \times 4$ couples quadrilaterals, and so on up to the $n \times n$ minor, the whole network. $W \succeq 0$ is that entire ladder at once, packed into a single convex constraint.

6. The real 2n × 2n lifting

$W$ is complex; SDP solvers speak real numbers. Split $W = W^{\mathrm{re}} + j\,W^{\mathrm{im}}$ and assemble the real matrix

\[M = \begin{bmatrix} W^{\mathrm{re}} & W^{\mathrm{im}} \\ -W^{\mathrm{im}} & W^{\mathrm{re}} \end{bmatrix} \succeq 0.\]

Because $W$ is Hermitian, $W^{\mathrm{re}}$ is symmetric and $W^{\mathrm{im}}$ is antisymmetric. The bottom-left block $-W^{\mathrm{im}}$ equals $(W^{\mathrm{im}})^{\top}$, so $M$ comes out symmetric, the form an SDP solver accepts. Testing $M \succeq 0$ is exactly testing $W \succeq 0$.

Proof: M is symmetric, and the two quadratic forms agree

Why $M$ is symmetric. Symmetry means the entry at $(p,q)$ equals the entry at $(q,p)$. Take an entry of the top-right block, row $i$, column $n+j$:

\[M[i,\, n+j] = W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij}.\]

The mirror entry, in the transposed position (row $n+j$, column $i$), lies in the bottom-left block $-W^{\mathrm{im}}$ at position $(j,i)$:

\[M[n+j,\, i] = (-W^{\mathrm{im}})_{ji} = -W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ji}.\]

Since $W^{\mathrm{im}}$ is antisymmetric, $W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ji} = -W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij}$, so

\[M[n+j,\, i] = -(-W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij}) = +W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij} = M[i,\, n+j].\]

Why $x^{*} W x = v^{\top} M v$. Write $x = a - j\,b$ (so $x^{*} = a^{\top} + j\,b^{\top}$), $W = W^{\mathrm{re}} + j\,W^{\mathrm{im}}$, and $v = (a;\, b)$.

Left side. First,

\[W x = (W^{\mathrm{re}} + j\,W^{\mathrm{im}})(a - j\,b) = \underbrace{(W^{\mathrm{re}}a + W^{\mathrm{im}}b)}_{R} + j\underbrace{(W^{\mathrm{im}}a - W^{\mathrm{re}}b)}_{I},\]

\[x^{*} W x = (a^{\top} + j\,b^{\top})(R + j\,I) = \underbrace{(a^{\top}R - b^{\top}I)}_{\text{real part}} + j\underbrace{(a^{\top}I + b^{\top}R)}_{\text{imaginary part}}.\]

The imaginary part dies. With $a^{\top}I = a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}a - a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}b$ and $b^{\top}R = b^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}a + b^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}b$, each surviving term is zero: $a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}a = 0$ and $b^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}b = 0$ by antisymmetry (a number equal to its own negative, since $a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}a = a^{\top}(W^{\mathrm{im}})^{\top}a = -a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}a$), and $-a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}b + b^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}a = 0$ because $b^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}a = a^{\top}(W^{\mathrm{re}})^{\top}b = a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}b$ (transposition rule $(XYZ)^{\top} = Z^{\top}Y^{\top}X^{\top}$ and $W^{\mathrm{re}}$ symmetric). The real part is then

\[x^{*} W x = a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}a + a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}b - b^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}a + b^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}b.\]

Right side. With $M$ and $v$ as above,

\[M v = \begin{bmatrix} W^{\mathrm{re}}a + W^{\mathrm{im}}b \\ -W^{\mathrm{im}}a + W^{\mathrm{re}}b \end{bmatrix},\]

\[v^{\top} M v = a^{\top}(W^{\mathrm{re}}a + W^{\mathrm{im}}b) + b^{\top}(-W^{\mathrm{im}}a + W^{\mathrm{re}}b) = a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}a + a^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}b - b^{\top}W^{\mathrm{im}}a + b^{\top}W^{\mathrm{re}}b.\]

Identical term by term. One is PSD if and only if the other is, so sending $M$ to the solver imposes the condition on $W$.

SDPWRM Formulation

SDPWRM reuses the shared generation, balance and objective blocks unchanged. What is specific to it is the pair of real voltage matrices WR_sdp/WI_sdp and the single positive-semidefinite constraint that couples them. The W-space branch flows are produced by the same add_socwr_branch_expressions! as SOCWR and QC, with R, T_var and v_magnitude_sq aliasing matrix entries.

Without storage the problem is a pure semidefinite program, which Lirion solves with Clarabel by default. The storage block adds a charge/discharge binary (Eq. 20), so a storage-bearing instance becomes a mixed-integer SDP and needs a solver that handles both the semidefinite cone and integer variables. The benchmark cases carry no storage.

Sets

  • $\mathcal{N}$: buses; $n = |\mathcal{N}|$. A fixed integer index $\operatorname{idx}(b) \in \{1,\dots,n\}$ places each bus in the matrices; the matrices $W^{\mathrm{re}}, W^{\mathrm{im}}$ span all $n$ buses.
  • $\mathcal{N}^{nv} \subseteq \mathcal{N}$: non-virtual buses, on which the power balance is enforced (non_virtual_bus_ids).
  • $\mathcal{L}$: active branches (in-service lines and transformers).
  • $\mathcal{G}$: generators; $\mathcal{G}^c \subseteq \mathcal{G}$ the curtailable subset.
  • $\mathcal{S}$: storage units.
  • $\mathcal{T} = \{1,\dots,T\}$: time periods.

Indices and incidence functions

  • $b, i, j \in \mathcal{N}$; $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$; $g \in \mathcal{G}$; $s \in \mathcal{S}$; $t \in \mathcal{T}$.
  • $f(\ell),\ t(\ell)$: from and to buses of branch $\ell$; their matrix rows/columns are $i_\ell = \operatorname{idx}(f(\ell))$ and $j_\ell = \operatorname{idx}(t(\ell))$.
  • $B(s)$: connection bus of storage $s$.

Parameters

Branches. $g_\ell, b_\ell$ (series conductance/susceptance); $g^{fr}_\ell, b^{fr}_\ell$ and $g^{to}_\ell, b^{to}_\ell$ (shunt, from / to sides); $k_\ell = 1/\tau_\ell$ the inverse tap ratio (inv_tap); $\tau^{re}_\ell = \tau_\ell\cos\Phi_\ell$ (tap_real) and $\tau^{im}_\ell = \tau_\ell\sin\Phi_\ell$ (tap_imag), the real/imaginary parts of the complex tap; $\overline{S}_\ell$ the apparent-flow limit (rate_a); $\theta^{\min}_\ell, \theta^{\max}_\ell$ the angular-aperture limits (ang_min, ang_max).

Buses. $V_b^{\min}, V_b^{\max}$ voltage-magnitude limits; $g^{sh}_b, b^{sh}_b$ shunt conductance/susceptance; $P^d_{b,t}, Q^d_{b,t}$ active/reactive demand.

Generators. $P_g^{\min}, P_g^{\max}$ and $Q_g^{\min}, Q_g^{\max}$ static limits; active/reactive time envelopes $\overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}, \underline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}$ and $\overline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t}, \underline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t}$; cost coefficients $c_{g,t,k}$; unit curtailment cost $c^{curt}_g$.

Storage. $E_s^{\min}, E_s^{\max}$ energy limits; initial energy $E^0_s$; charge/discharge power caps $C_s^{\max}, D_s^{\max}$; efficiencies $\eta^c_s, \eta^d_s$; converter resistance and reactance $r_s, x_s$; fixed losses $P^{loss}_s, Q^{loss}_s$; inverter thermal limit $\overline{S}_s$; reactive limits $Q_s^{\min}, Q_s^{\max}$.

Decision variables

Voltage (W-space). Two real matrices per period replace the magnitude/angle pair. There is no voltage-angle variable, and hence no reference-bus equation, since $W$ is invariant to a global phase rotation.

\[W^{\mathrm{re}}_t \in \mathbb{S}^{n}, \qquad W^{\mathrm{im}}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{n\times n}, \qquad t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{1}\]

$W^{\mathrm{re}}_t$ is declared symmetric in the code (Symmetric); $W^{\mathrm{im}}_t$ is forced antisymmetric by the PSD embedding (Eq. 13). The diagonal $W^{\mathrm{re}}_{t,ii}$ is the squared voltage magnitude of the bus at index $i$, exposed as the expression $w_{b,t} :=$ v_magnitude_sq $\,= W^{\mathrm{re}}_{t,\operatorname{idx}(b)\,\operatorname{idx}(b)}$. Both matrices carry a symmetry inherited from trigonometry:

  • $W^{\mathrm{re}}$ is symmetric: $W^{\mathrm{re}}_{ij} = v_i v_j \cos\theta_{ij}$; swapping $i \leftrightarrow j$ commutes the magnitudes and sends $\theta_{ji} = -\theta_{ij}$, but the cosine is even, so the value is unchanged.
  • $W^{\mathrm{im}}$ is antisymmetric: $W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij} = v_i v_j \sin\theta_{ij}$, and the sine is odd, so $W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ji} = -W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij}$; the diagonal is zero ($\sin 0 = 0$).

Generation.

\[p^g_{g,t} \in [P_g^{\min}, P_g^{\max}], \qquad Q^g_{g,t} \in [Q_g^{\min}, Q_g^{\max}], \qquad g\in\mathcal{G},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{2}\]

\[p^c_{g,t} \in [0, \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}], \qquad g\in\mathcal{G}^c,\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{3}\]

Storage (the W-space block shared with SOCWR).

\[E_{s,t}\in[E_s^{\min},E_s^{\max}],\quad c_{s,t}\in[0,C_s^{\max}],\quad d_{s,t}\in[0,D_s^{\max}],\quad P^s_{s,t}\in[-\overline{S}_s,\overline{S}_s], \tag{4}\]

\[Q^s_{s,t}\in[Q_s^{\min},Q_s^{\max}],\quad Q^{sc}_{s,t}\in[Q_s^{\min},Q_s^{\max}],\quad z_{s,t}\in\{0,1\},\quad \ell^{cm}_{s,t}\in\big[0,\ (\overline{S}_s/V^{\min})^2\big], \tag{5}\]

with $z_{s,t}$ the charge/discharge selector and $\ell^{cm}_{s,t}$ the squared converter current magnitude (ccms); $V^{\min}$ is a global voltage floor (model.ext[:v_min], default $0.9$).

Entry bounds

Diagonal, from the voltage limits:

\[(V_i^{\min})^2 \;\le\; W^{\mathrm{re}}_{ii} \;\le\; (V_i^{\max})^2.\]

Off-diagonal, from the voltage and angle limits, and only for pairs joined by a branch (a pair with no line carries no angle information; the entry exists, but is held only by the PSD constraint). These are the same three corner cases derived from the $W_r$, $W_i$ bounds of the QC (Quadratic Convex) (the corner principle, with the sign of the sine deciding the pairing).

If $\theta^{\min} \ge 0$:

\[V_i^{\min} V_j^{\min}\cos\theta^{\max} \;\le\; W^{\mathrm{re}}_{ij} \;\le\; V_i^{\max} V_j^{\max}\cos\theta^{\min},\]

\[V_i^{\min} V_j^{\min}\sin\theta^{\min} \;\le\; W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij} \;\le\; V_i^{\max} V_j^{\max}\sin\theta^{\max}.\]

If $\theta^{\max} \le 0$:

\[V_i^{\min} V_j^{\min}\cos\theta^{\min} \;\le\; W^{\mathrm{re}}_{ij} \;\le\; V_i^{\max} V_j^{\max}\cos\theta^{\max},\]

\[V_i^{\max} V_j^{\max}\sin\theta^{\min} \;\le\; W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij} \;\le\; V_i^{\min} V_j^{\min}\sin\theta^{\max}.\]

If the interval crosses zero:

\[V_i^{\min} V_j^{\min}\min\!\left(\cos\theta^{\min},\ \cos\theta^{\max}\right) \;\le\; W^{\mathrm{re}}_{ij} \;\le\; V_i^{\max} V_j^{\max},\]

\[V_i^{\max} V_j^{\max}\sin\theta^{\min} \;\le\; W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij} \;\le\; V_i^{\max} V_j^{\max}\sin\theta^{\max}.\]

Mirror. The other triangle of the matrix shares these bounds through the symmetries: $W^{\mathrm{re}}_{ji}$ inherits the same interval; $W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ji} = -W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij}$ inherits the reflected one (if $W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij} \in [a,b]$ then $W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ji} \in [-b,-a]$).

Parallel lines. If two or three branches join the same pair of buses, each brings its own limits but the matrix entry is a single one (indexing is by bus, not by branch). The resolution is the intersection of the intervals (the smallest of the ceilings, the largest of the floors). Each branch tightens the same entry, and the joint squeeze holds.

Objective function

The objective is the shared generation cost, identical to the exact AC Polar model: the fuel-cost polynomial evaluated on power in physical units ($S^{base}p^g$) plus, when curtailable generators are present, a linear curtailment cost.

\[F = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\sum_{k=1}^{m_{g,t}} c_{g,t,k}\,(S^{base}\,p^g_{g,t})^{m_{g,t}-k}, \qquad F^{curt} = \sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}^c}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}} c^{curt}_g\,S^{base}\,p^c_{g,t},\]

\[\min\; F + F^{curt}. \tag{6}\]

Branch flows

For branch $\ell$ write the aliased blocks: the two endpoint diagonals $W^{ff}_{\ell,t}=W^{\mathrm{re}}_{t,i_\ell i_\ell}$ and $W^{tt}_{\ell,t}=W^{\mathrm{re}}_{t,j_\ell j_\ell}$, and the off-diagonal pair $W^{r}_{\ell,t}=W^{\mathrm{re}}_{t,i_\ell j_\ell}$ (= R) and $W^{i}_{\ell,t}=W^{\mathrm{im}}_{t,i_\ell j_\ell}$ (= T_var). The four flows are linear in these blocks:

\[P^f_{\ell,t} = (g_\ell+g^{fr}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{ff}_{\ell,t} + (-g_\ell\tau^{re}_\ell + b_\ell\tau^{im}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{r}_{\ell,t} + (-b_\ell\tau^{re}_\ell - g_\ell\tau^{im}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{i}_{\ell,t}. \tag{7}\]

\[Q^f_{\ell,t} = -(b_\ell+b^{fr}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{ff}_{\ell,t} - (-b_\ell\tau^{re}_\ell - g_\ell\tau^{im}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{r}_{\ell,t} + (-g_\ell\tau^{re}_\ell + b_\ell\tau^{im}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{i}_{\ell,t}. \tag{8}\]

\[P^t_{\ell,t} = (g_\ell+g^{to}_\ell)\,W^{tt}_{\ell,t} + (-g_\ell\tau^{re}_\ell - b_\ell\tau^{im}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{r}_{\ell,t} - (-b_\ell\tau^{re}_\ell + g_\ell\tau^{im}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{i}_{\ell,t}. \tag{9}\]

\[Q^t_{\ell,t} = -(b_\ell+b^{to}_\ell)\,W^{tt}_{\ell,t} - (-b_\ell\tau^{re}_\ell + g_\ell\tau^{im}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{r}_{\ell,t} - (-g_\ell\tau^{re}_\ell - b_\ell\tau^{im}_\ell)\,k_\ell^2\,W^{i}_{\ell,t}. \tag{10}\]

Note the inverse tap $k_\ell^2$ scales only the from-side self term (Eq. 7, 8) and all cross terms; the to-side self term (Eq. 9, 10) carries no tap factor.

Power balance

At each non-virtual bus, injections meet withdrawals; the shunt draws on the diagonal block $w_{b,t}=W^{\mathrm{re}}_{t,\operatorname{idx}(b)\,\operatorname{idx}(b)}$.

Active power.

\[\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}(b)} p^g_{g,t} - \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}(b)} P^s_{s,t} - P^d_{b,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,f(\ell)=b} P^f_{\ell,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,t(\ell)=b} P^t_{\ell,t} - g^{sh}_b\,w_{b,t} = 0, \qquad b\in\mathcal{N}^{nv},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{11}\]

Reactive power.

\[\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}(b)} Q^g_{g,t} - \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}(b)} Q^s_{s,t} - Q^d_{b,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,f(\ell)=b} Q^f_{\ell,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,t(\ell)=b} Q^t_{\ell,t} + b^{sh}_b\,w_{b,t} = 0, \qquad b\in\mathcal{N}^{nv},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{12}\]

The two shunt terms carry opposite signs, as they do throughout Lirion: the conductance draws active power ($-g^{sh}_b w_{b,t}$), while the stored susceptance (pf.shunt_susceptance) enters reactive balance with a plus ($+b^{sh}_b w_{b,t}$).

Positive-semidefinite constraint

The single condition that defines the relaxation. The code assembles one global $2n\times 2n$ real symmetric block over all $n$ buses (no clique or chordal decomposition) and passes it to the solver, per period:

\[M_t = \begin{bmatrix} W^{\mathrm{re}}_t & W^{\mathrm{im}}_t \\ -W^{\mathrm{im}}_t & W^{\mathrm{re}}_t \end{bmatrix} \succeq 0, \qquad t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{13}\]

Requiring $M_t$ symmetric is what forces $W^{\mathrm{im}}_t$ antisymmetric; testing $M_t \succeq 0$ is exactly testing the Hermitian $W_t = W^{\mathrm{re}}_t + j\,W^{\mathrm{im}}_t \succeq 0$ (Section 6 of the derivation). This global block absorbs the per-pair $2\times 2$ Jabr cone of SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation), which is therefore not posted separately here.

Branch thermal limits

Two second-order cones per branch, on the from- and to-side flows:

\[(\overline{S}_\ell,\ P^f_{\ell,t},\ Q^f_{\ell,t}) \in \text{SecondOrderCone}, \qquad (\overline{S}_\ell,\ P^t_{\ell,t},\ Q^t_{\ell,t}) \in \text{SecondOrderCone}, \qquad \ell\in\mathcal{L},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{14}\]

Angle limits

A linear cut on the blocks (there is no $\theta$ variable, so no raw angle-difference constraint is imposed, unlike SOCWR). Each side is posted only where its own aperture limit is set: the upper cut where $\theta^{\max}_\ell$ is present, the lower cut where $\theta^{\min}_\ell$ is present:

\[\tan(\theta^{\min}_\ell)\,W^{r}_{\ell,t} \;\le\; W^{i}_{\ell,t} \;\le\; \tan(\theta^{\max}_\ell)\,W^{r}_{\ell,t}, \qquad \ell\in\mathcal{L},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{15}\]

The tangent argument is clipped to $\pm(\pi/2 - 10^{-6})$ (_tan_clip) so the slope stays finite as the aperture approaches $\pm 90^\circ$.

Lifted nonlinear cuts (LNC)

The same two cuts per branch as SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) (Coffrin–Hijazi–Van Hentenryck 2015), added only when the aperture is finite, lies within $\pm\pi/2$ and satisfies $\theta^{\min}_\ell<\theta^{\max}_\ell$. With $\phi=(\theta^{\max}_\ell+\theta^{\min}_\ell)/2$, $d=(\theta^{\max}_\ell-\theta^{\min}_\ell)/2$, $s_f=V^{\min}_f+V^{\max}_f$ and $s_t=V^{\min}_t+V^{\max}_t$:

\[s_f s_t\,(\cos\phi\,W^{r}_{\ell,t}+\sin\phi\,W^{i}_{\ell,t}) - V^{\max}_t\cos d\,s_t\,W^{ff}_{\ell,t} - V^{\max}_f\cos d\,s_f\,W^{tt}_{\ell,t} \;\ge\; V^{\max}_f V^{\max}_t\cos d\,(V^{\min}_f V^{\min}_t - V^{\max}_f V^{\max}_t). \tag{16}\]

\[s_f s_t\,(\cos\phi\,W^{r}_{\ell,t}+\sin\phi\,W^{i}_{\ell,t}) - V^{\min}_t\cos d\,s_t\,W^{ff}_{\ell,t} - V^{\min}_f\cos d\,s_f\,W^{tt}_{\ell,t} \;\ge\; -V^{\min}_f V^{\min}_t\cos d\,(V^{\min}_f V^{\min}_t - V^{\max}_f V^{\max}_t). \tag{17}\]

They are kept because the PSD ladder knows the structure (the blocks of a common generator) but not the voltage and angle limits. No minor carries that information, and the LNC cuts inject it. Accumulation, as always: each piece cuts a slack the others do not see.

Generation constraints

Active/reactive time envelopes are added only where a period limit is strictly tighter than the static bound. The per-period upper active bound is skipped for renewables, which take the curtailment equality instead; the lower active bound and the reactive envelope apply to every generator.

\[\underline{P}^{ts}_{g,t} \le p^g_{g,t} \le \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}\ (g\notin\mathcal{G}^c), \quad \underline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t} \le Q^g_{g,t} \le \overline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t}, \quad p^g_{g,t}+p^c_{g,t}=\overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}\ (g\in\mathcal{G}^c). \tag{18}\]

Storage constraints (W-space)

SDPWRM reuses the SOCWR storage model. Storage dispatches to the W-space block add_socwr_storage_constraints!. Non-simultaneity is the binary big-M pair (Eq. 20), not the bilinear $c\,d = 0$ of AC Polar, and the converter-current bound rests on the single global $V^{\min}$ floor. Energy balance:

\[E_{s,1} = E^0_s + \eta^c_s c_{s,1} - \frac{d_{s,1}}{\eta^d_s}, \qquad E_{s,t} = E_{s,t-1} + \eta^c_s c_{s,t} - \frac{d_{s,t}}{\eta^d_s}\ (t\ge 2), \qquad s\in\mathcal{S}. \tag{19}\]

Charge/discharge exclusivity (binary big-M by the ramp caps):

\[c_{s,t} \le z_{s,t}\,C_s^{\max}, \qquad d_{s,t} \le (1-z_{s,t})\,D_s^{\max}, \qquad s\in\mathcal{S},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{20}\]

Converter thermal limit (a rotated cone through the connection-bus diagonal $w_{B(s),t}$) and the active/reactive couplings in the current-magnitude variable $\ell^{cm}_{s,t}$:

\[(P^s_{s,t})^2 + (Q^s_{s,t})^2 \;\le\; w_{B(s),t}\,\ell^{cm}_{s,t}, \qquad s\in\mathcal{S},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{21}\]

\[P^s_{s,t} + (d_{s,t}-c_{s,t}) = P^{loss}_s + r_s\,\ell^{cm}_{s,t}, \qquad Q^s_{s,t} = Q^{sc}_{s,t} + Q^{loss}_s + x_s\,\ell^{cm}_{s,t}, \qquad s\in\mathcal{S},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{22}\]

\[\min_{\substack{W^{\mathrm{re}},\,W^{\mathrm{im}},\,p^g,\,Q^g,\,p^c,\\ E,\,c,\,d,\,P^s,\,Q^s,\,Q^{sc},\,z,\,\ell^{cm}}} \quad F + F^{curt} \qquad (\text{Eq. } 6)\]

What it captures / what it loses

Philosophy

Captures: a single convex tie that couples every loop at once. The ladder of principal minors runs from the $2 \times 2$ SOCWR cone through the $3 \times 3$ loop block up to the whole $n \times n$ network, so SDPWRM is never looser than SOCWR and can be strictly tighter wherever a loop is present. What the tie does not do is force the loop angles to sum to zero at every feasible point. It pins a cycle exactly only when its branches sit on the SOCWR cone boundary (the unit-modulus case of rung 3), and in full generality only in the rank-one limit of the next bullet. A feasible $W \succeq 0$ in the cone interior can still carry loop angles that do not close.

Loses: the rank-one requirement. A feasible $W \succeq 0$ need not factor as $V V^{*}$, so it may match no genuine set of bus voltages. The optimum is a lower bound on the true AC cost, not a point the network can actually run.

Lirion vs. the original

Warning

The semidefinite program is handled on the real symmetric lifting $M$ (the $2n \times 2n$ matrix of Section 6), which is the standard way to pass a Hermitian PSD constraint to a real SDP solver. Lirion assembles one dense $2n \times 2n$ block over all $n$ buses and does not exploit chordal sparsity, the decomposition into smaller overlapping cliques that large-scale SDP-OPF codes lean on. The dense form stays faithful to the whole matrix and keeps the model simple, at the cost of how large a network solves in reasonable time. The convex semidefinite relaxation of the OPF is due to Bai, Wei, Fujisawa and Wang (2008), who first cast the problem over a semidefinite voltage matrix. Lavaei and Low (2012) later gave conditions under which the relaxation is exact (zero duality gap).

Worked example

The guiding example runs throughout: three buses in a triangle (branches 1-2, 2-3, 3-1).

The lie SOCWR cannot see

All three buses have magnitude $v = 1$. Suppose the optimizer picks the same blocks on every branch:

branch$W_r$$W_i$implicit angle $\theta_{ij} = \operatorname{atan2}(W_i, W_r)$
1-2$\cos 30^\circ = 0.866$$\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$$30^\circ$
2-3$0.866$$0.5$$30^\circ$
3-1$0.866$$0.5$$30^\circ$

Test each branch against the SOCWR cone: $W_r^2 + W_i^2 = 0.866^2 + 0.5^2 = 1 = W_{ii}W_{jj}$. All three pass, with perfect equality (they look like genuine voltages!). But sum the angles around the loop. For any genuine bus angles the differences telescope to zero, $(\theta_1 - \theta_2) + (\theta_2 - \theta_3) + (\theta_3 - \theta_1) = 0$; here they would sum to $30^\circ + 30^\circ + 30^\circ = 90^\circ \ne 0$. No three bus angles produce three $30^\circ$ differences around a triangle. SOCWR accepted a point that no physical network realizes, and no per-branch squeeze can bar it, because each branch in isolation is impeccable.

Hand the same blocks to the SDP and it refuses them. With every magnitude $1$ the three cross-entries are $W_{12} = W_{23} = W_{31} = e^{j30^\circ}$, so the $3\times 3$ matrix is exactly the unit-modulus triangle of rung 3 with $\alpha = \beta = \gamma = 30^\circ$ and $\xi = 90^\circ$. Its determinant is $2(\cos 90^\circ - 1) = -2$, which is negative, so the matrix is not positive semidefinite. The SDP throws out the very point SOCWR waved through.

The same triangle, genuine voltages

Take genuine voltages $V_1 = 1$, $V_2 = e^{-j30^\circ}$, $V_3 = e^{-j60^\circ}$ (bus angles $0^\circ$, $-30^\circ$, $-60^\circ$, so the differences are $30^\circ$ on branches 1-2 and 2-3 and $-60^\circ$ on 3-1, closing the loop):

\[V = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ e^{-j30^\circ} \\ e^{-j60^\circ} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad V^{*} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & e^{j30^\circ} & e^{j60^\circ} \end{bmatrix},\]

\[W = V\,V^{*} = \begin{bmatrix} V_1 V_1^{*} & V_1 V_2^{*} & V_1 V_3^{*} \\ V_2 V_1^{*} & V_2 V_2^{*} & V_2 V_3^{*} \\ V_3 V_1^{*} & V_3 V_2^{*} & V_3 V_3^{*} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & e^{j30^\circ} & e^{j60^\circ} \\ e^{-j30^\circ} & 1 & e^{j30^\circ} \\ e^{-j60^\circ} & e^{-j30^\circ} & 1 \end{bmatrix}.\]

Because this $W$ is built as $V V^{*}$ it is rank one, so the loop closes on its own. This is the honest counterpart of the lie above: the same triangle, but with angles that come from three real bus phasors.

A fully worked three-bus instance

Data: $V_1 = 1.00\angle 0^\circ$, $V_2 = 0.95\angle{-10^\circ}$, $V_3 = 1.05\angle{-25^\circ}$. Angle differences and magnitude products:

pair$v_i v_j$$\theta_{ij} = \theta_i - \theta_j$$\cos\theta_{ij}$$\sin\theta_{ij}$
1-2$1.00 \times 0.95 = 0.95$$0 - (-10) = 10^\circ$$0.98481$$0.17365$
1-3$1.00 \times 1.05 = 1.05$$0 - (-25) = 25^\circ$$0.90631$$0.42262$
2-3$0.95 \times 1.05 = 0.9975$$-10 - (-25) = 15^\circ$$0.96593$$0.25882$

(The cycle checks out: $10^\circ + 15^\circ + (-25^\circ) = 0$.)

Build $W^{\mathrm{re}}$ (with $W^{\mathrm{re}}_{ij} = v_i v_j \cos\theta_{ij}$ and $W^{\mathrm{re}}_{ii} = v_i^2$) and $W^{\mathrm{im}}$ (with $W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ij} = v_i v_j \sin\theta_{ij}$ and $W^{\mathrm{im}}_{ii} = \sin 0 = 0$):

\[W^{\mathrm{re}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.00000 & 0.93557 & 0.95162 \\ 0.93557 & 0.90250 & 0.96351 \\ 0.95162 & 0.96351 & 1.10250 \end{bmatrix} \ (\text{symmetric}), \qquad W^{\mathrm{im}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0.16497 & 0.44375 \\ -0.16497 & 0 & 0.25817 \\ -0.44375 & -0.25817 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \ (\text{antisymmetric}).\]

The two together give the complex $W = W^{\mathrm{re}} + j\,W^{\mathrm{im}}$, entry by entry:

\[W = \begin{bmatrix} 1.00000 & 0.93557 + j\,0.16497 & 0.95162 + j\,0.44375 \\ 0.93557 - j\,0.16497 & 0.90250 & 0.96351 + j\,0.25817 \\ 0.95162 - j\,0.44375 & 0.96351 - j\,0.25817 & 1.10250 \end{bmatrix}.\]

Finally the real $6 \times 6$ matrix that goes to the solver, $M = \begin{bmatrix} W^{\mathrm{re}} & W^{\mathrm{im}} \\ -W^{\mathrm{im}} & W^{\mathrm{re}} \end{bmatrix}$:

\[M = \left(\begin{array}{ccc|ccc} 1.00000 & 0.93557 & 0.95162 & 0 & 0.16497 & 0.44375 \\ 0.93557 & 0.90250 & 0.96351 & -0.16497 & 0 & 0.25817 \\ 0.95162 & 0.96351 & 1.10250 & -0.44375 & -0.25817 & 0 \\ \hline 0 & -0.16497 & -0.44375 & 1.00000 & 0.93557 & 0.95162 \\ 0.16497 & 0 & -0.25817 & 0.93557 & 0.90250 & 0.96351 \\ 0.44375 & 0.25817 & 0 & 0.95162 & 0.96351 & 1.10250 \end{array}\right).\]

The bottom-left block is $W^{\mathrm{im}}$ with every sign flipped which, since $W^{\mathrm{im}}$ is already antisymmetric, equals $(W^{\mathrm{im}})^{\top}$. The whole $M$ comes out symmetric, the format the SDP solver accepts.

QC (Quadratic Convex)

Quadratic-Convex relaxation, recursive McCormick (QCRM).

The QC model is a convex relaxation of the AC power flow in W-space, built on the same four flow expressions as SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) (which in turn reuses the rectangular AC Rectangular (ACR) flows). The difference is where the convexity comes from. SOCWR treats the cross-blocks $W_r$, $W_i$ as opaque variables tied from the outside by a single rotated cone. The QC relaxation attacks from the inside. It opens the internal structure of each block and relaxes every piece separately: the magnitude product, the cosine, the sine, and the products that rebuild the blocks. In this variant QC does not carry the Jabr voltage-product cone at all. It trades that one cone for the inside family, so the two relaxations are different rather than nested. Its full name is QCRM, Quadratic-Convex Recursive-McCormick, recursive because the blocks are nested products (a product of a product), each wrapped in its own McCormick envelope [4].

Relaxation or approximation?

QC is a convex relaxation: it enlarges the feasible set, so its optimum is a guaranteed lower bound on the true AC optimum. It is a different relaxation from SOCWR, not a strict tightening of it. QC drops the Jabr voltage-product cone and fences each piece of the blocks on its own, so the two feasible sets are not nested in general. See Foundations.

Derivation

Intermediate variables

Recall what the real block $W_r$ actually is:

\[W_r = \underbrace{v_i\,v_j}_{\text{product of magnitudes}} \cdot \underbrace{\cos\theta_{ij}}_{\text{cosine of the angle}}.\]

SOCWR never looked inside this expression. The QC relaxation decomposes it: it creates a variable for each piece and fences each piece with its own convex relaxation. To do so it introduces the following intermediate variables (the identifiers are the ones used in the code):

VariableRepresents
td$\theta_i - \theta_j$
vv$v_i \cdot v_j$
csenvelope of $\cos\theta_{ij}$
sienvelope of $\sin\theta_{ij}$
ccmsquared branch current

The bus variables are magnitude, angle, and the lifted squares $W_{ii}$ and $W_{jj}$ (the from- and to-bus squared magnitudes). The branch variables are $W_r$ and $W_i$. The idea is to rebuild the blocks as a chain of products:

\[W_r = \mathrm{vv}\cdot\mathrm{cs}, \qquad W_i = \mathrm{vv}\cdot\mathrm{si}, \qquad \mathrm{vv} = v_i\cdot v_j.\]

Branch flows

The QC flows are exactly those of SOCWR, which in turn were those of the ACR model. With $tm = |T|^2 = \tau^2$ the squared tap magnitude and $t_r$, $t_i$ the real and imaginary parts of the complex tap $T$:

\[p_{ij} = \frac{(g + g^{fr})\,W_{ii}}{tm} + \frac{(-g\,t_r + b\,t_i)\,W_r}{tm} + \frac{(-b\,t_r - g\,t_i)\,W_i}{tm},\]

\[q_{ij} = -\frac{(b + b^{fr})}{tm}\,W_{ii} - \frac{(-b\,t_r - g\,t_i)}{tm}\,W_r + \frac{(-g\,t_r + b\,t_i)}{tm}\,W_i,\]

\[p_{ji} = (g + g^{to})\,W_{jj} + \frac{(-g\,t_r - b\,t_i)}{tm}\,W_r + \frac{(b\,t_r - g\,t_i)}{tm}\,W_i,\]

\[q_{ji} = -(b + b^{to})\,W_{jj} - \frac{(-b\,t_r + g\,t_i)}{tm}\,W_r + \frac{(g\,t_r + b\,t_i)}{tm}\,W_i.\]

Bounds on the intermediate variables

Before anything is relaxed, each new variable needs bounds, because McCormick requires bounds on the factors.

td. Directly from the branch angle limits:

\[\theta^{\min} \le \mathrm{td} \le \theta^{\max}.\]

vv. A product of two positive factors, so minimum with minimums, maximum with maximums:

\[v_i^{\min}\,v_j^{\min} \le \mathrm{vv} \le v_i^{\max}\,v_j^{\max}.\]

si. The sine is increasing on $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$ (its derivative, the cosine, is positive there); an increasing function maps the endpoints of the interval onto the endpoints of the range (a single case):

\[\sin\theta^{\min} \le \mathrm{si} \le \sin\theta^{\max}.\]

cs. The cosine is not monotone on intervals that cross zero (it rises on the left, falls on the right, peaks at zero), so its bounds depend on where the interval lies:

\[\begin{aligned} \theta^{\min} \ge 0 &:\quad \cos\theta^{\max} \le \mathrm{cs} \le \cos\theta^{\min},\\ \theta^{\max} \le 0 &:\quad \cos\theta^{\min} \le \mathrm{cs} \le \cos\theta^{\max},\\ \text{crosses zero} &:\quad \min(\cos\theta^{\min}, \cos\theta^{\max}) \le \mathrm{cs} \le 1. \end{aligned}\]

wr, wi. The magnitudes ride along, $W_r = v_i v_j \cos\theta_{ij}$ and $W_i = v_i v_j \sin\theta_{ij}$ are products $a\cdot s$ with $a = v_i v_j \ge 0$. The cosine is $\ge 0$ on $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$; the sine changes sign between cases:

\[\begin{aligned} \theta^{\min} \ge 0 &:\quad v_i^{\min} v_j^{\min}\cos\theta^{\max} \le W_r \le v_i^{\max} v_j^{\max}\cos\theta^{\min},\\ &\phantom{:}\quad v_i^{\min} v_j^{\min}\sin\theta^{\min} \le W_i \le v_i^{\max} v_j^{\max}\sin\theta^{\max};\\[4pt] \theta^{\max} \le 0 &:\quad v_i^{\min} v_j^{\min}\cos\theta^{\min} \le W_r \le v_i^{\max} v_j^{\max}\cos\theta^{\max},\\ &\phantom{:}\quad v_i^{\max} v_j^{\max}\sin\theta^{\min} \le W_i \le v_i^{\min} v_j^{\min}\sin\theta^{\max};\\[4pt] \text{crosses zero} &:\quad v_i^{\min} v_j^{\min}\min(\cos\theta^{\min}, \cos\theta^{\max}) \le W_r \le v_i^{\max} v_j^{\max},\\ &\phantom{:}\quad v_i^{\max} v_j^{\max}\sin\theta^{\min} \le W_i \le v_i^{\max} v_j^{\max}\sin\theta^{\max}. \end{aligned}\]

$\mathrm{ccm}$ is deliberately left out of this section: its bounds only make sense once it is defined (Constraint 8).

Constraints

Each constraint is numbered continuously within the model.

Constraint 1: secant of the magnitude

SOCWR and QC promoted $W_{ii}$ to an independent variable: it is no longer computed from $v_i$ but chosen by the optimizer. A free $W_{ii}$ can lie. The optimizer may pick a value that does not correspond to any true $v_i^2$. We therefore need constraints that pin $W_{ii}$ near $v_i^2$. The true relation is not convex as an equality:

\[W_{ii} = v_i^2\]

is a parabola, and equality with a curve is non-convex. Instead of forcing $W_{ii}$ onto the parabola, we trap it between a floor and a ceiling.

Floor. $W_{ii} \ge v_i^2$, easy and convex (the region above a parabola is the interior of a bowl, which is convex).

Ceiling. $W_{ii} \le v_i^2$ would mark the region below the parabola, which is non-convex (the exterior of the bowl). So the parabola cannot be used as a ceiling. A line can: below a line is a half-plane, always convex. The line must (i) stay above the parabola on all of $[v_i^{\min}, v_i^{\max}]$ (otherwise it would cut valid physical points) and (ii) be as low as possible (the tighter the band, the better the relaxation). The secant through the two endpoints of the parabola satisfies both. Its endpoints are $P_1 = (v_i^{\min}, (v_i^{\min})^2)$ and $P_2 = (v_i^{\max}, (v_i^{\max})^2)$, so the slope is

\[m = \frac{(v_i^{\max})^2 - (v_i^{\min})^2}{v_i^{\max} - v_i^{\min}} = v_i^{\max} + v_i^{\min},\]

and the secant line reads

\[W_{ii} = (v_i^{\min} + v_i^{\max})\,v_i - v_i^{\min}\,v_i^{\max}.\]

The complete band:

\[v_i^2 \le W_{ii} \le (v_i^{\min} + v_i^{\max})\,v_i - v_i^{\min}\,v_i^{\max}.\]

Constraint 2: exact angle coupling

\[\theta_i - \theta_j = \mathrm{td}.\]

An exact, linear equality. This is the piece SOCWR did not have: there the blocks lived loose, with no link to the bus angles. Here the bus angle $\theta$ ties the branch difference $\mathrm{td}$.

This restores angle cycle consistency

Because $\mathrm{td}$ comes from $\theta_i - \theta_j$ and all envelopes rest on $\mathrm{td}$, the angle differences telescope around any loop: $\sum \mathrm{td} = 0$ on a cycle, automatically, since each $\theta_i$ is a single number. This returns to QC the angle cycle consistency that SOCWR lost. The blocks $W_r, W_i$ still reach those angles only through the relaxed envelopes, so phasor consistency itself stays relaxed.

Constraint 3: cosine envelope

We want $\mathrm{cs} = \cos(\mathrm{td})$, but equality with a curve is non-convex. We fence $\mathrm{cs}$ between a convex floor and a convex ceiling. Since the cosine is concave on $[-\pi/2, \pi/2]$, concavity fixes the roles: the ceiling is a curve above, the floor is a secant below. Define $m = \max(|\theta^{\min}|, |\theta^{\max}|)$.

Ceiling (the parabola). Take an inverted parabola with its peak where the cosine peaks. The cosine peaks at $\mathrm{td} = 0$ with value $\cos 0 = 1$, so the parabola must have peak $1$ at $\mathrm{td} = 0$. From the general form $C_s(\mathrm{td}) = a\,\mathrm{td}^2 + b\,\mathrm{td} + c$, imposing $C_s(0) = 1$ gives $c = 1$, and a zero slope at the peak ($C_s'(\mathrm{td}) = 2a\,\mathrm{td} + b$, $C_s'(0) = b = 0$) leaves

\[C_s = 1 - k\,\mathrm{td}^2, \qquad k > 0.\]

The coefficient $k$ is fixed by requiring the parabola to touch the cosine at the ends $\mathrm{td} = \pm m$: as low as it can go while still touching both tips (any larger $k$ would cut the cosine; any smaller $k$ would leave it higher and looser). At $\mathrm{td} = m$ the cosine is $\cos m$, so

\[1 - k\,m^2 = \cos m \;\Longrightarrow\; k = \frac{1 - \cos m}{m^2},\]

and the ceiling is

\[\mathrm{cs} \le 1 - \frac{1 - \cos m}{m^2}\,\mathrm{td}^2.\]

The expression for $k$ is used only for $m > 0$. When the aperture collapses to a point ($m = 0$, no angle freedom on the branch) the envelope degenerates and $\mathrm{cs} = 1$ is fixed.

The two curves coincide at three points, $\mathrm{td} \in \{-m, 0, +m\}$. That they touch at the tips does not by itself guarantee the parabola stays above in between. The proof is below.

Proof that the parabola envelops the cosine

Define the gap

\[f(\mathrm{td}) = \left(1 - \frac{1 - \cos m}{m^2}\,\mathrm{td}^2\right) - \cos(\mathrm{td}),\]

and show $f(\mathrm{td}) \ge 0$ for all $\mathrm{td} \in [-m, m]$. By construction $f$ vanishes at three points: $f(0) = 0$ (both equal $1$), $f(m) = 0$ (both equal $\cos m$) and $f(-m) = 0$ (by symmetry, both functions are even). The question is the sign of $f$ between these zeros. Differentiate twice:

\[f'(\mathrm{td}) = -2k\,\mathrm{td} + \sin(\mathrm{td}), \qquad f''(\mathrm{td}) = \cos(\mathrm{td}) - 2k.\]

The term $\cos(\mathrm{td})$ ranges between $\cos m$ (at the tips) and $1$ (at the centre), while $2k = 2(1-\cos m)/m^2$ is a constant. We locate $2k$ by proving, for $m \in (0, \pi/2]$,

\[\cos m < \frac{2(1 - \cos m)}{m^2} < 1.\]

Part 1 ($2k < 1$). This is $1 - \cos m < m^2/2$. From the geometric fact $\sin x < x$ for $x > 0$ (the vertical segment is shorter than the arc on the unit circle), integrate from $0$ to $m$:

\[\int_0^m \sin x\,dx < \int_0^m x\,dx \;\Longrightarrow\; 1 - \cos m < \frac{m^2}{2}.\]

Part 2 ($2k > \cos m$). This is $2(1 - \cos m) > m^2 \cos m$. Define $g(m) = 2(1 - \cos m) - m^2 \cos m$ with $g(0) = 0$ and differentiate:

\[g'(m) = 2\sin m - \big(2m\cos m - m^2 \sin m\big) = \underbrace{2(\sin m - m\cos m)}_{A} + \underbrace{m^2 \sin m}_{B}.\]

$B > 0$ for $0 < m \le \pi/2$. For $A$, set $h(m) = \sin m - m\cos m$ with $h(0) = 0$; then $h'(m) = \cos m - (\cos m - m\sin m) = m\sin m > 0$ on $0 < m < \pi/2$, so $h$ starts at zero and grows, giving $A > 0$. With $A > 0$ and $B > 0$, $g' > 0$: $g$ starts at zero and grows, so $g(m) > 0$.

Conclusion. With $\cos m < 2k < 1$ located, the sign of $f'' = \cos(\mathrm{td}) - 2k$ changes once inside the interval: near the centre ($\cos\mathrm{td}$ close to $1 > 2k$) we have $f'' > 0$, and $f$ leaves the zero at $\mathrm{td} = 0$ curving upward; near the tips ($\cos\mathrm{td}$ close to $\cos m < 2k$) we have $f'' < 0$, and $f$ comes back down to zero at $\pm m$ from above. It rises from the central zero, falls to the tip zeros, and never crosses: $f \ge 0$ on all of $[-m, m]$. The parabola envelops the cosine. ∎

Floor (the secant). The line through the two endpoints of the cosine on the interval, $P_1 = (\theta^{\min}, \cos\theta^{\min})$ and $P_2 = (\theta^{\max}, \cos\theta^{\max})$, with slope $m_{\text{sec}} = (\cos\theta^{\max} - \cos\theta^{\min})/(\theta^{\max} - \theta^{\min})$:

\[\mathrm{cs} \ge \frac{\cos\theta^{\max} - \cos\theta^{\min}}{\theta^{\max} - \theta^{\min}} \,(\mathrm{td} - \theta^{\min}) + \cos\theta^{\min}.\]

The complete frame: secant $\le \mathrm{cs} \le$ inverted parabola.

Constraint 4: sine envelope

The sine is nearly linear near zero and, unlike the cosine, changes concavity in the middle of the window: it is concave for $\theta > 0$, convex for $\theta < 0$, with an inflection at $\theta = 0$. Because the envelope technique (a curve on one side, a secant on the other) needs a fixed concavity, the sine splits into three cases. The tool is the tangent line: since $\frac{d}{d\theta}\sin\theta = \cos\theta$, the tangent at $(\alpha, \sin\alpha)$ is

\[\mathrm{si} = \cos(\alpha)\,(\mathrm{td} - \alpha) + \sin\alpha.\]

A concave function lies below every one of its tangents; a convex one lies above. With $m = \max(|\theta^{\min}|, |\theta^{\max}|)$:

Case 1 (interval all positive, $\theta^{\min} \ge 0$). The sine is concave throughout; the envelope matches the cosine's. Floor = secant (concave ⇒ chord below the curve); ceiling = tangent at $\alpha = m/2$ (concave ⇒ tangent above the curve):

\[\mathrm{si} \le \cos(m/2)\,(\mathrm{td} - m/2) + \sin(m/2).\]

The arc centre $m/2$ minimizes the worst gap, but the tangent is valid at any point on the concave side.

Case 2 (interval all negative, $\theta^{\max} \le 0$). The sine is convex throughout, so ceiling and floor swap. Ceiling = secant (convex ⇒ chord above); floor = tangent at $\alpha = -m/2$ (convex ⇒ tangent below):

\[\mathrm{si} \le \frac{\sin\theta^{\min} - \sin\theta^{\max}}{\theta^{\min} - \theta^{\max}} \,(\mathrm{td} - \theta^{\min}) + \sin\theta^{\min},\]

\[\mathrm{si} \ge \cos(m/2)\,(\mathrm{td} + m/2) - \sin(m/2).\]

Case 3 (interval crosses zero, $\theta^{\min} < 0 < \theta^{\max}$). The interval contains the inflection, so no secant is guaranteed on either side; use two tangents, one per side:

\[\mathrm{si} \le \cos(m/2)\,(\mathrm{td} - m/2) + \sin(m/2) \quad \text{(tangent at } +m/2,\ \text{ceiling)},\]

\[\mathrm{si} \ge \cos(m/2)\,(\mathrm{td} + m/2) - \sin(m/2) \quad \text{(tangent at } -m/2,\ \text{floor)}.\]

The tangent on the concave side binds from above, the one on the convex side from below, and each stays valid across the whole interval.

Proof of the tangent bound

Take the ceiling $T(\mathrm{td}) = \cos(\alpha)\,(\mathrm{td} - \alpha) + \sin\alpha$ with $\alpha = m/2$.

Step 1 (gap function).

\[\varphi(\mathrm{td}) = T(\mathrm{td}) - \sin(\mathrm{td}).\]

We want $\varphi \ge 0$ on all of $[-m, m]$. By construction $\varphi(\alpha) = 0$ and $\varphi'(\alpha) = 0$.

Step 2 (derivative of the gap).

\[\varphi'(\mathrm{td}) = \cos(\alpha) - \cos(\mathrm{td}).\]

This is a comparison of cosines; since the cosine is even and decreasing on $[0, \pi/2]$, $\cos(\mathrm{td}) = \cos(|\mathrm{td}|)$ and the comparison depends only on the distance to zero:

\[|\mathrm{td}| < \alpha \;\to\; \cos(\mathrm{td}) > \cos(\alpha) \;\to\; \varphi' < 0, \qquad |\mathrm{td}| > \alpha \;\to\; \varphi' > 0.\]

Step 3 (the gap map). The sign forces the shape of $\varphi$ on $[-m, m]$: rising on $[-m, -\alpha]$, falling on $[-\alpha, \alpha]$, rising on $[\alpha, m]$, an up, down, up profile. A function of this shape attains its minimum only at the end of the descent ($\mathrm{td} = \alpha$, where $\varphi = 0$) or at the left tip $\mathrm{td} = -m$; everything else is above these two:

\[\min_{[-m,\,m]} \varphi = \min\{\varphi(-m),\, 0\}.\]

Step 4 (the left tip is non-negative). Only $\varphi(-m) \ge 0$ remains. Write $u = m/2$ and use $\sin m = 2\sin u\cos u$ to factor out the positive cosine:

\[\varphi(-m) = \sin u + \sin 2u - 3u\cos u = \cos u\,\big(\tan u + 2\sin u - 3u\big).\]

Let $h(u) = \tan u + 2\sin u - 3u$. Then $h(0) = 0$ and $h'(u) = \sec^2 u + 2\cos u - 3$ with $h'(0) = 0$; differentiating once more,

\[h''(u) = 2\sec^2 u\,\tan u - 2\sin u = 2\sin u\,\frac{1 - \cos^3 u}{\cos^3 u} > 0 \quad\text{on } \left(0, \tfrac{\pi}{4}\right].\]

So $h'$ rises from $0$ and stays positive, $h$ rises from $0$ and stays positive, and $\varphi(-m) = \cos u\,h(u) \ge 0$. Hence $\varphi \ge 0$ on all of $[-m, m]$: the tangent at $m/2$ stays above the sine across the window. The all-negative case is its mirror image, the floor tangent at $-m/2$ bounding the sine from below. ∎

Constraint 5: McCormick products (the full envelope)

For a product $z = x\cdot y$ with $x \in [x^{\min}, x^{\max}]$ and $y \in [y^{\min}, y^{\max}]$, the complete envelope is four linear inequalities, each born from a product of two factors of known sign:

\[\begin{aligned} \text{Floor 1 (both at minimum)}: &\quad z \ge x^{\min} y + y^{\min} x - x^{\min} y^{\min},\\ \text{Floor 2 (both at maximum)}: &\quad z \ge x^{\max} y + y^{\max} x - x^{\max} y^{\max},\\ \text{Ceiling 1 (opposite sides)}: &\quad z \le x^{\min} y + y^{\max} x - x^{\min} y^{\max},\\ \text{Ceiling 2 (opposite sides)}: &\quad z \le x^{\max} y + y^{\min} x - x^{\max} y^{\min}. \end{aligned}\]

Together they are the convex hull of the graph of $z = xy$ over the box $[x^{\min},x^{\max}]\times[y^{\min},y^{\max}]$, the tightest convex envelope of a single bilinear term. QC instantiates this envelope three times, the recursive part, because the output of one product feeds the bounds of the next.

Product 1, $\mathrm{vv} = v_i\cdot v_j$ ($z = \mathrm{vv}$, $x = v_i$, $y = v_j$):

\[\begin{aligned} \mathrm{vv} &\ge v_i^{\min} v_j + v_j^{\min} v_i - v_i^{\min} v_j^{\min},\\ \mathrm{vv} &\ge v_i^{\max} v_j + v_j^{\max} v_i - v_i^{\max} v_j^{\max},\\ \mathrm{vv} &\le v_i^{\min} v_j + v_j^{\max} v_i - v_i^{\min} v_j^{\max},\\ \mathrm{vv} &\le v_i^{\max} v_j + v_j^{\min} v_i - v_i^{\max} v_j^{\min}. \end{aligned}\]

Product 2, $W_r = \mathrm{vv}\cdot\mathrm{cs}$ ($z = W_r$, $x = \mathrm{vv}$, $y = \mathrm{cs}$), with $\mathrm{vv} \in [\mathrm{vv}^{\min}, \mathrm{vv}^{\max}]$ ($\mathrm{vv}^{\min} = v_i^{\min} v_j^{\min}$, $\mathrm{vv}^{\max} = v_i^{\max} v_j^{\max}$) and $\mathrm{cs} \in [\mathrm{cs}^{\min}, \mathrm{cs}^{\max}]$ (the cosine-envelope bounds, the three cases above):

\[\begin{aligned} W_r &\ge \mathrm{vv}^{\min}\,\mathrm{cs} + \mathrm{cs}^{\min}\,\mathrm{vv} - \mathrm{vv}^{\min}\,\mathrm{cs}^{\min},\\ W_r &\ge \mathrm{vv}^{\max}\,\mathrm{cs} + \mathrm{cs}^{\max}\,\mathrm{vv} - \mathrm{vv}^{\max}\,\mathrm{cs}^{\max},\\ W_r &\le \mathrm{vv}^{\min}\,\mathrm{cs} + \mathrm{cs}^{\max}\,\mathrm{vv} - \mathrm{vv}^{\min}\,\mathrm{cs}^{\max},\\ W_r &\le \mathrm{vv}^{\max}\,\mathrm{cs} + \mathrm{cs}^{\min}\,\mathrm{vv} - \mathrm{vv}^{\max}\,\mathrm{cs}^{\min}. \end{aligned}\]

The $\mathrm{vv}$ that enters here is itself already tied by the four cuts of Product 1.

Product 3, $W_i = \mathrm{vv}\cdot\mathrm{si}$ ($z = W_i$, $x = \mathrm{vv}$, $y = \mathrm{si}$), with $\mathrm{si} \in [\sin\theta^{\min}, \sin\theta^{\max}]$:

\[\begin{aligned} W_i &\ge \mathrm{vv}^{\min}\,\mathrm{si} + \mathrm{si}^{\min}\,\mathrm{vv} - \mathrm{vv}^{\min}\,\mathrm{si}^{\min},\\ W_i &\ge \mathrm{vv}^{\max}\,\mathrm{si} + \mathrm{si}^{\max}\,\mathrm{vv} - \mathrm{vv}^{\max}\,\mathrm{si}^{\max},\\ W_i &\le \mathrm{vv}^{\min}\,\mathrm{si} + \mathrm{si}^{\max}\,\mathrm{vv} - \mathrm{vv}^{\min}\,\mathrm{si}^{\max},\\ W_i &\le \mathrm{vv}^{\max}\,\mathrm{si} + \mathrm{si}^{\min}\,\mathrm{vv} - \mathrm{vv}^{\max}\,\mathrm{si}^{\min}. \end{aligned}\]

This is the full recursive-McCormick chain: nested products, each with its envelope of four, the $\mathrm{cs}$ and $\mathrm{si}$ envelopes feeding the bounds.

Constraint 6: thermal limit

\[p_{ij}^2 + q_{ij}^2 \le \mathrm{rate}_a^2, \qquad p_{ji}^2 + q_{ji}^2 \le \mathrm{rate}_a^2.\]

Constraint 7: angle limits via tangent

\[\tan(\theta^{\min})\,W_r \le W_i \le \tan(\theta^{\max})\,W_r.\]

The angle limit becomes a linear cut on the blocks. It is valid because $W_r > 0$ in the physical region ($v_i v_j > 0$ and $\cos\theta_{ij} > 0$ for $|\theta_{ij}| < \pi/2$).

Constraint 8: the squared current (ccm)

Motivation. The optimizer might find blocks $W_r, W_i, W_{ii}, W_{jj}$ and flows $p, q$ that satisfy the thermal limit yet correspond to no coherent current. The physical current–voltage–power relation is one more tie the relaxation can exploit; QC injects it through a new variable.

The branch model. Recall the branch (see AC Polar): an ideal transformer on the from end, an internal node at voltage $V' = V_i/T$, a series element $y$ and a local shunt $y^{fr} = g_{fr} + j\,b_{fr}$. The total current entering the internal node is

\[I' = \underbrace{y\,(V' - V_j)}_{I_{\text{series}}} + \underbrace{y^{fr}\,V'}_{I_{\text{shunt}}}.\]

Define

\[\mathrm{ccm} := |I'|^2\]

(the squared current at the internal node; since $I_i = I'/T^*$, it equals $\tau^2 |I_i|^2$, with $\tau = |T|$).

Step 1 (the bridge with the flows). By ideal-transformer conservation, $S_{ij} = V_i I_i^* = V' I'^*$; taking magnitudes, $|S_{ij}|^2 = |V'|^2 |I'|^2$. With $|S_{ij}|^2 = p_{ij}^2 + q_{ij}^2$ and $|V'|^2 = |V_i|^2/\tau^2 = W_{ii}/tm$, this is physically the equality $p_{ij}^2 + q_{ij}^2 = (W_{ii}/tm)\,\mathrm{ccm}$. Imposing it exactly would bring back non-convexity (a product of variables on one side), so we relax it:

\[p_{ij}^2 + q_{ij}^2 \le \frac{W_{ii}}{tm}\,\mathrm{ccm},\]

a rotated second-order cone, the same geometry as the Jabr tie in SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation).

Step 2, expressing ccm in the blocks (series current). On its own, Step 1 says nothing ($\mathrm{ccm}$ could inflate freely); the force comes from the algebraic equation tying it to the blocks. Start with the series current:

\[|I_{\text{series}}|^2 = |y|^2\,|V' - V_j|^2 = (g^2 + b^2)\Big(|V'|^2 + |V_j|^2 - 2\,\mathrm{Re}(V' V_j^*)\Big).\]

Open each piece in the blocks: $|V'|^2 = W_{ii}/tm$, $|V_j|^2 = W_{jj}$, and, using $V_i V_j^* = W_r + j\,W_i$ and $1/T = T^*/|T|^2 = (t_r - j\,t_i)/tm$,

\[\mathrm{Re}(V' V_j^*) = \mathrm{Re}\!\left(\frac{V_i V_j^*}{T}\right) = \frac{t_r\,W_r + t_i\,W_i}{tm}.\]

Collecting,

\[|I_{\text{series}}|^2 = (g^2 + b^2)\left(\frac{W_{ii}}{tm} + W_{jj} - \frac{2\,(t_r\,W_r + t_i\,W_i)}{tm}\right).\]

Linear in the blocks, and this is why the computation works: the squared series current, which looked quadratic, is linear in $W_{ii}, W_{jj}, W_r, W_i$.

Step 3 (the shunt correction). $\mathrm{ccm}$ is $|I'|^2$, not $|I_{\text{series}}|^2$. From $S_{ij} = V'(I')^*$ we get $I' = S_{ij}^*/(V')^*$, and $I_{\text{series}} = I' - y^{fr} V'$. Expanding $|A - B|^2 = |A|^2 - 2\,\mathrm{Re}(AB^*) + |B|^2$ with $A = S_{ij}^*/(V')^*$ and $B = y^{fr} V'$:

  • $|A|^2 = |S_{ij}|^2/|V'|^2 = \mathrm{ccm}$,
  • $|B|^2 = |y^{fr}|^2 |V'|^2 = (g_{fr}^2 + b_{fr}^2)\,W_{ii}/tm$,
  • $AB^* = S_{ij}^*\,(y^{fr})^* = (S_{ij}\,y^{fr})^*$, so $\mathrm{Re}(AB^*) = \mathrm{Re}(S_{ij}\,y^{fr}) = g_{fr}\,p_{ij} - b_{fr}\,q_{ij}$.

Hence

\[|I_{\text{series}}|^2 = \mathrm{ccm} - 2\,(g_{fr}\,p_{ij} - b_{fr}\,q_{ij}) + (g_{fr}^2 + b_{fr}^2)\,\frac{W_{ii}}{tm}.\]

Isolating $\mathrm{ccm}$ and substituting Step 2 gives the closed form:

\[\mathrm{ccm} = (g^2 + b^2)\left(\frac{W_{ii}}{tm} + W_{jj} - \frac{2\,(t_r\,W_r + t_i\,W_i)}{tm}\right) - (g_{fr}^2 + b_{fr}^2)\,\frac{W_{ii}}{tm} + 2\,(g_{fr}\,p_{ij} - b_{fr}\,q_{ij}).\]

Ceiling of ccm. From $|S_{ij}| \le \mathrm{rate}_a$ and $|S_{ij}| = |V'|\,|I'|$:

\[|I'| = \frac{|S_{ij}|}{|V'|} \le \frac{\mathrm{rate}_a}{v_i^{\min}/\tau} = \frac{\mathrm{rate}_a\,\tau}{v_i^{\min}} \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathrm{ccm} \le \left(\frac{\mathrm{rate}_a\,\tau}{v_i^{\min}}\right)^2.\]

Constraint 9: Lifted Nonlinear Cuts (LNC)

The QC model in Lirion keeps the same two LNC cuts as SOCWR. Their defining quantities are

\[\phi = \frac{\theta^{\max} + \theta^{\min}}{2}, \qquad d = \frac{\theta^{\max} - \theta^{\min}}{2},\]

\[s_f = v_f^{\min} + v_f^{\max}, \qquad s_t = v_t^{\min} + v_t^{\max}.\]

The two LNC cuts come from the SOCWR page

QC posts the same pair of lifted nonlinear cuts as SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation), on the shared $W_r, W_i$ blocks. They are derived in full there (the McCormick bridge and the explicit Cut 1 / Cut 2), so this page cross-references them rather than repeating the construction.

QC Formulation

The formulation below collects, in consolidated form and without re-deriving, exactly the blocks the model builds. The derivation above justifies each piece. The model is assembled as an NLP and solved with Ipopt.

Sets

  • $\mathcal{N}$: set of buses.
  • $\mathcal{N}^{nv} \subseteq \mathcal{N}$: non-virtual buses, on which the power balance is enforced (non_virtual_bus_ids).
  • $\mathcal{L}$: set of active branches (in-service lines and transformers).
  • $\mathcal{P}$: set of ordered $(f,t)$ bus pairs carrying at least one active branch. The cross-blocks $W_r, W_i$ live on this set, one product per pair, shared by all parallel branches (add_rt_variables_with_bounds!).
  • $\mathcal{G}$: set of existing generators; $\mathcal{G}^c \subseteq \mathcal{G}$: curtailable (renewable) generators.
  • $\mathcal{S}$: set of storage units.
  • $\mathcal{T} = \{1,\dots,T\}$: time periods.

Indices and incidence functions

  • $b, i, j \in \mathcal{N}$; $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$; $p \in \mathcal{P}$; $g \in \mathcal{G}$; $s \in \mathcal{S}$; $t \in \mathcal{T}$.
  • $f(\ell),\ t(\ell)$: from and to buses of branch $\ell$.
  • $p(\ell) \in \mathcal{P}$: the bus pair of branch $\ell$ ($\mathrm{pair\_of}$ in the code); $W_r, W_i$ of $\ell$ are the pair variables of $p(\ell)$.
  • $B(s)$: connection bus of storage $s$.

Parameters

Branches.

  • $g_\ell,\ b_\ell$: series conductance and susceptance (g, b).
  • $g^{fr}_\ell,\ b^{fr}_\ell$; $g^{to}_\ell,\ b^{to}_\ell$: shunt conductance/susceptance of the from and to ends (g_fr,b_fr,g_to,b_to).
  • $\tau_\ell$: tap magnitude (tap_position); $k_\ell = 1/\tau_\ell$ (inv_tap); $\Phi_\ell$: phase shift (tap_angle).
  • $t_r = \tau_\ell\cos\Phi_\ell$ (tap_real), $t_i = \tau_\ell\sin\Phi_\ell$ (tap_imag); $tm_\ell = |T_\ell|^2 = \tau_\ell^2$ the squared tap magnitude. Every tap denominator on this page is $1/tm_\ell$, which the code writes as $\texttt{inv\_tap}^2$ in the flows and as $1/\texttt{tap\_position}^2$ in the current tie.
  • $\mathrm{rate}_{a,\ell}$: branch apparent-power (thermal) limit (rate_a).
  • $\delta_\ell^{\min},\ \delta_\ell^{\max}$: angular-aperture limits (ang_min, ang_max), written $\theta^{\min},\theta^{\max}$ in the derivation above.

Buses.

  • $V_b^{\min},\ V_b^{\max}$: voltage-magnitude limits.
  • $g^{sh}_b,\ b^{sh}_b$: bus shunt conductance and susceptance.
  • $P^d_{b,t},\ Q^d_{b,t}$: active and reactive demand.

Generators. As in AC Polar: $P_g^{\min/\max}$, $Q_g^{\min/\max}$, the active/reactive time envelopes $\overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}, \underline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}$, $\overline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t}, \underline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t}$, the cost coefficients $c_{g,t,k}$ and the unit curtailment cost $c^{curt}_g$.

Storage. As in AC Polar: $E_s^{\min/\max}$, $E^0_s$, charge/discharge ramps $C_s^{\max}, D_s^{\max}$, efficiencies $\eta^c_s, \eta^d_s$, converter $r_s, x_s$, fixed losses $P^{loss}_s, Q^{loss}_s$, inverter limit $\overline{S}_s$ (thermal_limit), and $Q_s^{\min/\max}$. The $\mathrm{ccm}^s$ ceiling uses a single global minimum voltage $V^{\min}$ (model.ext[:v_min], default $0.9$), not the per-bus bound.

Decision variables

Voltage / W-space.

\[\theta_{b,t} \in \mathbb{R} \;\text{: voltage angle at bus } b. \tag{1}\]

\[V_{b,t} \in [V_b^{\min}, V_b^{\max}] \;\text{: voltage magnitude.} \tag{2}\]

\[W_{b,t} \in [(V_b^{\min})^2, (V_b^{\max})^2] \;\text{: lifted squared magnitude } v_b^2 \ (\texttt{v\_magnitude\_sq}). \tag{3}\]

Branch / bus-pair blocks. The cross-blocks are per bus pair $p$; the rest are per branch $\ell$. For $\ell$ write $W_r \equiv W^r_{p(\ell),t}$, $W_i \equiv W^i_{p(\ell),t}$, $W_{ii} \equiv W_{f(\ell),t}$, $W_{jj} \equiv W_{t(\ell),t}$.

\[W^r_{p,t} \in [W^{r,\min}_p, W^{r,\max}_p],\qquad W^i_{p,t} \in [W^{i,\min}_p, W^{i,\max}_p] \;\text{: cross blocks } v_iv_j\cos\theta_{ij},\ v_iv_j\sin\theta_{ij}. \tag{4}\]

\[\mathrm{td}_{\ell,t} \in [\delta_\ell^{\min}, \delta_\ell^{\max}] \;\text{: branch angle difference.} \tag{5}\]

\[\mathrm{vv}_{\ell,t} \in [V_i^{\min}V_j^{\min}, V_i^{\max}V_j^{\max}] \;\text{: magnitude product } v_iv_j. \tag{6}\]

\[\mathrm{cs}_{\ell,t} \in [\mathrm{cs}^{\min}_\ell, \mathrm{cs}^{\max}_\ell] \;\text{: cosine envelope,} \qquad \mathrm{si}_{\ell,t} \in [\sin\delta_\ell^{\min}, \sin\delta_\ell^{\max}] \;\text{: sine envelope,} \tag{7}\]

with the cosine box set by the three-case rule ($\delta^{\min}\!\ge 0$: $[\cos\delta^{\max},\cos\delta^{\min}]$; $\delta^{\max}\!\le 0$: $[\cos\delta^{\min},\cos\delta^{\max}]$; crossing zero: $[\min(\cos\delta^{\min},\cos\delta^{\max}),\,1]$).

\[\mathrm{ccm}_{\ell,t} \in \Big[0,\ \big(\mathrm{rate}_{a,\ell}\,\tau_\ell / V_i^{\min}\big)^2\Big] \;\text{: squared internal-node current.} \tag{8}\]

The $W_r, W_i$ pair bounds are the PowerModels voltage-product bounds computed per bus pair on the tightest aggregated aperture ($\max$ of $\delta^{\min}$, $\min$ of $\delta^{\max}$ across parallel branches), the per-pair version of the $W_r, W_i$ bounds derived above (_rt_pair_product_bounds). The $\mathrm{ccm}$ ceiling is applied only when $\mathrm{rate}_{a,\ell}$ is finite and positive.

Generation.

\[p^g_{g,t} \in [P_g^{\min}, P_g^{\max}],\qquad Q^g_{g,t} \in [Q_g^{\min}, Q_g^{\max}],\qquad p^c_{g,t} \in [0, \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}]\ (g\in\mathcal{G}^c). \tag{9}\]

Storage (identical to the SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) W-space storage model, see the note under Storage constraints).

\[E_{s,t}\in[E_s^{\min},E_s^{\max}],\ c_{s,t}\in[0,C_s^{\max}],\ d_{s,t}\in[0,D_s^{\max}],\ P^s_{s,t}\in[-\overline{S}_s,\overline{S}_s], \tag{10}\]

\[Q^s_{s,t}\in[Q_s^{\min},Q_s^{\max}],\ Q^{sc}_{s,t}\in[Q_s^{\min},Q_s^{\max}],\ \mathrm{ccm}^s_{s,t}\in\big[0,(\overline{S}_s/V^{\min})^2\big],\ z_{s,t}\in\{0,1\}. \tag{11}\]

With storage present, the binary $z_{s,t}$ lifts the instance to a mixed-integer NLP. Without storage, QC is the pure NLP solved directly by Ipopt.

Objective function

Identical to AC Polar (src/modeling/objective/generation_cost.jl): the fuel cost on physical power $S^{base}p^g$ plus the curtailment cost when curtailable units exist.

\[F = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\sum_{k=1}^{m_{g,t}} c_{g,t,k}\,(S^{base}p^g_{g,t})^{m_{g,t}-k}, \qquad F^{curt} = \sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}^c}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}} c^{curt}_g\,S^{base}\,p^c_{g,t}, \tag{12}\]

\[\min\; F + F^{curt}. \tag{13}\]

Branch flows (W-space)

The four flows are the SOCWR/ACR expressions, reused verbatim (add_socwr_branch_expressions!). The branch angle difference is tied to the bus angles. QC has no separate $\delta^{\min}\le\theta_i-\theta_j\le\delta^{\max}$ constraint. That limit is enforced by the bound (5) on $\mathrm{td}$ together with the exact coupling:

\[\theta_{f(\ell),t} - \theta_{t(\ell),t} = \mathrm{td}_{\ell,t}, \qquad \ell\in\mathcal{L},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{14}\]

Here $\mathrm{td}$ is the raw angle difference $\theta_{f(\ell)}-\theta_{t(\ell)}$, with no phase-shift term; the transformer phase $\Phi_\ell$ enters only through $t_r, t_i$ in the flow coefficients below.

\[P^f_{\ell,t} = \frac{(g_\ell+g^{fr}_\ell)}{tm_\ell}\,W_{ii} + \frac{(-g_\ell t_r + b_\ell t_i)}{tm_\ell}\,W_r + \frac{(-b_\ell t_r - g_\ell t_i)}{tm_\ell}\,W_i, \tag{15}\]

\[Q^f_{\ell,t} = -\frac{(b_\ell+b^{fr}_\ell)}{tm_\ell}\,W_{ii} - \frac{(-b_\ell t_r - g_\ell t_i)}{tm_\ell}\,W_r + \frac{(-g_\ell t_r + b_\ell t_i)}{tm_\ell}\,W_i, \tag{16}\]

\[P^t_{\ell,t} = (g_\ell+g^{to}_\ell)\,W_{jj} + \frac{(-g_\ell t_r - b_\ell t_i)}{tm_\ell}\,W_r + \frac{(b_\ell t_r - g_\ell t_i)}{tm_\ell}\,W_i, \tag{17}\]

\[Q^t_{\ell,t} = -(b_\ell+b^{to}_\ell)\,W_{jj} - \frac{(-b_\ell t_r + g_\ell t_i)}{tm_\ell}\,W_r + \frac{(g_\ell t_r + b_\ell t_i)}{tm_\ell}\,W_i. \tag{18}\]

Power balance

At each non-virtual bus (non_virtual_bus_ids), the shunt term rides on the lifted square $W_{b,t}$ (not $V_{b,t}^2$):

\[\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}(b)} p^g_{g,t} - \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}(b)} P^s_{s,t} - P^d_{b,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,f(\ell)=b} P^f_{\ell,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,t(\ell)=b} P^t_{\ell,t} - g^{sh}_b\,W_{b,t} = 0, \qquad b\in\mathcal{N}^{nv},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{19}\]

\[\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}(b)} Q^g_{g,t} - \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}(b)} Q^s_{s,t} - Q^d_{b,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,f(\ell)=b} Q^f_{\ell,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,t(\ell)=b} Q^t_{\ell,t} + b^{sh}_b\,W_{b,t} = 0, \qquad b\in\mathcal{N}^{nv},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{20}\]

Reference bus

\[\theta_{ref,t} = 0, \qquad t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{21}\]

Magnitude lifting (secant band)

For every bus (add_qc_bus_variables!): parabolic floor and secant ceiling (Constraint 1):

\[V_{b,t}^2 \le W_{b,t} \le (V_b^{\min}+V_b^{\max})\,V_{b,t} - V_b^{\min}V_b^{\max}, \qquad b\in\mathcal{N},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{22}\]

Trigonometric envelopes

Cosine (Constraint 3), quadratic ceiling and secant floor, with $m = \max(|\delta^{\min}_\ell|,|\delta^{\max}_\ell|)$:

\[\mathrm{cs}_{\ell,t} \le 1 - \frac{1-\cos m}{m^2}\,\mathrm{td}_{\ell,t}^2, \tag{23}\]

\[\mathrm{cs}_{\ell,t} \ge \frac{\cos\delta^{\min}_\ell - \cos\delta^{\max}_\ell}{\delta^{\min}_\ell - \delta^{\max}_\ell} \,(\mathrm{td}_{\ell,t} - \delta^{\min}_\ell) + \cos\delta^{\min}_\ell. \tag{24}\]

Sine (Constraint 4), three cases on the sign of the aperture, with the same $m$. Crossing zero ($\delta^{\min}<0<\delta^{\max}$): two tangents. All negative ($\delta^{\max}\le 0$): secant ceiling, tangent floor. All positive ($\delta^{\min}\ge 0$): tangent ceiling, secant floor.

\[\begin{aligned} \text{crosses }0:\quad &\mathrm{si} \le \cos\tfrac{m}{2}\big(\mathrm{td}-\tfrac{m}{2}\big)+\sin\tfrac{m}{2}, &&\mathrm{si} \ge \cos\tfrac{m}{2}\big(\mathrm{td}+\tfrac{m}{2}\big)-\sin\tfrac{m}{2};\\[2pt] \delta^{\max}\!\le 0:\quad &\mathrm{si} \le \tfrac{\sin\delta^{\min}-\sin\delta^{\max}}{\delta^{\min}-\delta^{\max}} (\mathrm{td}-\delta^{\min})+\sin\delta^{\min}, &&\mathrm{si} \ge \cos\tfrac{m}{2}\big(\mathrm{td}+\tfrac{m}{2}\big)-\sin\tfrac{m}{2};\\[2pt] \delta^{\min}\!\ge 0:\quad &\mathrm{si} \le \cos\tfrac{m}{2}\big(\mathrm{td}-\tfrac{m}{2}\big)+\sin\tfrac{m}{2}, &&\mathrm{si} \ge \tfrac{\sin\delta^{\min}-\sin\delta^{\max}}{\delta^{\min}-\delta^{\max}} (\mathrm{td}-\delta^{\min})+\sin\delta^{\min}. \end{aligned} \tag{25}\]

McCormick products

For a product $z=x\,y$ with $x\in[\underline{x},\overline{x}]$, $y\in[\underline{y},\overline{y}]$ the four envelope cuts are

\[\begin{aligned} z &\ge \underline{x}\,y + \underline{y}\,x - \underline{x}\,\underline{y}, & z &\ge \overline{x}\,y + \overline{y}\,x - \overline{x}\,\overline{y},\\ z &\le \underline{x}\,y + \overline{y}\,x - \underline{x}\,\overline{y}, & z &\le \overline{x}\,y + \underline{y}\,x - \overline{x}\,\underline{y}. \end{aligned} \tag{26}\]

Instantiated three times (the recursive chain), in order, each per branch $\ell$ and time $t$:

#$z$$x$ (box)$y$ (box)
Product 1$\mathrm{vv}_{\ell,t}$$V_{i,t}\in[V_i^{\min},V_i^{\max}]$$V_{j,t}\in[V_j^{\min},V_j^{\max}]$
Product 2$W_r$$\mathrm{vv}_{\ell,t}\in[V_i^{\min}V_j^{\min},V_i^{\max}V_j^{\max}]$$\mathrm{cs}_{\ell,t}\in[\mathrm{cs}^{\min},\mathrm{cs}^{\max}]$
Product 3$W_i$$\mathrm{vv}_{\ell,t}$ (same box)$\mathrm{si}_{\ell,t}\in[\sin\delta^{\min},\sin\delta^{\max}]$

Branch thermal limits

Posted as quadratic inequalities (QC is an NLP; unlike SOCWR these are not conic constraints):

\[(P^f_{\ell,t})^2 + (Q^f_{\ell,t})^2 \le \mathrm{rate}_{a,\ell}^2,\qquad (P^t_{\ell,t})^2 + (Q^t_{\ell,t})^2 \le \mathrm{rate}_{a,\ell}^2, \qquad \ell\in\mathcal{L},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{27}\]

Angle-difference tangent cuts

On the cross-blocks (Constraint 7), with the tangent argument clipped to $\pm(\pi/2-10^{-6})$; each cut is posted only when the corresponding limit is not NaN:

\[\tan(\delta^{\min}_\ell)\,W_r \le W_i \le \tan(\delta^{\max}_\ell)\,W_r, \qquad \ell\in\mathcal{L},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{28}\]

Lifted nonlinear cuts (LNC)

The same two cuts as SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) (add_qc_lnc_cuts!), posted per branch only when $\delta^{\min}_\ell,\delta^{\max}_\ell$ are non-NaN, both lie in $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ and $\delta^{\min}_\ell<\delta^{\max}_\ell$. With $\phi=\tfrac{\delta^{\max}+\delta^{\min}}{2}$, $d=\tfrac{\delta^{\max}-\delta^{\min}}{2}$, $s_f=V_f^{\min}+V_f^{\max}$, $s_t=V_t^{\min}+V_t^{\max}$:

\[s_f s_t(\cos\phi\,W_r+\sin\phi\,W_i) - V_t^{\max}\cos d\,s_t\,W_{ii} - V_f^{\max}\cos d\,s_f\,W_{jj} \ge V_f^{\max}V_t^{\max}\cos d\,(V_f^{\min}V_t^{\min}-V_f^{\max}V_t^{\max}), \tag{29}\]

\[s_f s_t(\cos\phi\,W_r+\sin\phi\,W_i) - V_t^{\min}\cos d\,s_t\,W_{ii} - V_f^{\min}\cos d\,s_f\,W_{jj} \ge -V_f^{\min}V_t^{\min}\cos d\,(V_f^{\min}V_t^{\min}-V_f^{\max}V_t^{\max}). \tag{30}\]

Current-magnitude tie (ccm)

The rotated-cone link and the algebraic closed form (Constraint 8), per branch and time:

\[(P^f_{\ell,t})^2 + (Q^f_{\ell,t})^2 \le \frac{W_{ii}}{tm_\ell}\,\mathrm{ccm}_{\ell,t}, \tag{31}\]

\[\mathrm{ccm}_{\ell,t} = (g_\ell^2+b_\ell^2)\Big(\frac{W_{ii}}{tm_\ell} + W_{jj} - \frac{2(t_r W_r + t_i W_i)}{tm_\ell}\Big) - (g^{fr}_\ell{}^2 + b^{fr}_\ell{}^2)\,\frac{W_{ii}}{tm_\ell} + 2\,(g^{fr}_\ell P^f_{\ell,t} - b^{fr}_\ell Q^f_{\ell,t}). \tag{32}\]

Generation constraints

The active/reactive time envelopes (added only where strictly tighter than the scalar bound) and the renewable curtailment equality, as in AC Polar:

\[p^g_{g,t} \le \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}\ (g\in\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{G}^c),\qquad p^g_{g,t} \ge \underline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}\ (g\in\mathcal{G}),\qquad \underline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t} \le Q^g_{g,t} \le \overline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t}\ (g\in\mathcal{G}). \tag{33}\]

\[p^g_{g,t} + p^c_{g,t} = \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}, \qquad g\in\mathcal{G}^c,\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{34}\]

Storage constraints

QC storage is the SOCWR W-space model (not the AC Polar one)

QC borrows the SOCWR storage model. When a case carries storage, QC dispatches to add_socwr_storage_constraints! (storage/dispatch.jl), so storage is relaxed in W-space: non-simultaneity is a binary big-M (not the bilinear $cd=0$ of AC Polar), and the inverter limit is a rotated cone on a squared-current variable $\mathrm{ccm}^s$ (not the constant-$\overline{S}_s^2$ quadratic).

Energy balance (_storage_energy_balance!):

\[E_{s,1} = E^0_s + \eta^c_s c_{s,1} - \frac{d_{s,1}}{\eta^d_s},\qquad E_{s,t} = E_{s,t-1} + \eta^c_s c_{s,t} - \frac{d_{s,t}}{\eta^d_s}\ (t\ge 2). \tag{35}\]

Non-simultaneous charge/discharge via the binary $z_{s,t}$:

\[c_{s,t} \le z_{s,t}\,C_s^{\max}, \qquad d_{s,t} \le (1-z_{s,t})\,D_s^{\max}. \tag{36}\]

Inverter limit (rotated cone) and converter AC coupling:

\[(P^s_{s,t})^2 + (Q^s_{s,t})^2 \le W_{B(s),t}\,\mathrm{ccm}^s_{s,t}, \tag{37}\]

\[P^s_{s,t} + (d_{s,t} - c_{s,t}) = P^{loss}_s + r_s\,\mathrm{ccm}^s_{s,t}, \qquad Q^s_{s,t} = Q^{sc}_{s,t} + Q^{loss}_s + x_s\,\mathrm{ccm}^s_{s,t}. \tag{38}\]

What it captures / what it loses

Philosophy

Captures. By opening the blocks, QC adds (i) the exact angle coupling $\mathrm{td} = \theta_i - \theta_j$, which restores angle cycle consistency (the bus angles telescope to $\sum\mathrm{td} = 0$ around any loop), and (ii) per-piece envelopes (secant/parabola on $\mathrm{cs}$, tangents on $\mathrm{si}$, recursive McCormick on the products, plus the squared-current tie $\mathrm{ccm}$) in place of SOCWR's single voltage-product cone. Neither relaxation dominates the other in general.

Loses. The link from $\mathrm{td}$ to the blocks passes through relaxed envelopes, so the net only closes as far as the envelopes squeeze. QC remains a per-branch (per-bus-pair) relaxation: it does not impose the network-wide, rank-1 condition that SDPWRM places on the whole voltage matrix.

Lirion vs. the original

Warning

Lirion implements the recursive-McCormick (QCRM) variant of the quadratic-convex relaxation of Hijazi, Coffrin and Van Hentenryck (2017), as provided in PowerModels [4]. Two implementation notes:

  • The cross-blocks $W_r, W_i$ (R, T_var in the code) are shared per bus-pair: parallel branches alias one product and each posts its McCormick chain onto that shared pair, which is a valid, tighter-or-equal relaxation.
  • The relaxation is assembled as an NLP, keeping the thermal limit (Constraint 6) as a quadratic constraint and the power-magnitude tie (Constraint 8) as a rotated-cone quadratic solved with Ipopt, rather than a purely conic or linear form. The LNC cuts (Constraint 9) are the lifted nonlinear cuts of Coffrin, Hijazi and Van Hentenryck (2015), the same block as the SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) model (also posted by SDPWRM).

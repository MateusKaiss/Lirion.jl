LPAC

The DC model delivered the cheapest possible solve, but at the price of an amputation: no voltage magnitude, no reactive power, no losses. For transmission operation under normal conditions this may suffice; for any study involving reactive support, voltage limits, or a more heavily loaded network, the model simply does not see the phenomenon. The LPAC model (Linear Programming AC) meets that gap with an intermediate question: can voltage and reactive power be given back to the model without giving back the non-convexity? The answer is yes, and the mechanism is a change of variable. Instead of working with the magnitude $v_i$, LPAC works with its deviation from nominal.

Approximation, not relaxation

Like the DC model, LPAC is an approximation, not a relaxation: its optimum is neither a floor nor a ceiling on the true AC cost. But it errs far less, and over a much wider range of operating regimes.

The voltage-deviation variable

For each bus, define

\[\phi_i := v_i - 1,\]

the deviation of the magnitude from its nominal of $1$ pu.

Why does this substitution help? In normal operation $v_i$ lies between $0.95$ and $1.05$, so $\phi_i$ is a small number, on the order of $\pm 0.05$. And a small number has a useful property: its square is negligible ($0.05^2 = 0.0025$, twenty times smaller). It is this asymmetry that lets the flow expressions be linearized without significant error. The nonlinearity of the magnitude products concentrates in the $\phi^2$ terms, and they vanish. Compare with the choice made by the DC model, which was more radical: there $v_i = 1$, i.e. $\phi_i = 0$, fixed. LPAC leaves $\phi_i$ free as a variable and treats only its products as negligible. One extra variable per bus, and reactive power returns to the model. Reactive power rides on the voltage magnitudes, the very decoupling the DC model invoked when it dropped $q$. Giving the magnitude back as a free variable is what lets reactive power act again.

Derivation: three linearizations over the W-space flows

The structure that organizes the whole model is this. Recall the flows of the SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) and QC (Quadratic Convex) models, which are linear in the $W$ blocks:

\[p_{ij} = \frac{g + g^{fr}}{tm}\,w_{ii} + \frac{-g\,t_r + b\,t_i}{tm}\,w_r + \frac{-b\,t_r - g\,t_i}{tm}\,w_i .\]

LPAC reuses these formulas wholesale. It replaces each block by an expression linear in $\phi$ and $\theta$. Three substitutions, one per block.

This is the mirror image of the W-space relaxations. SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) and QC (Quadratic Convex) kept the blocks as free variables and fenced them with a cone or with envelopes, enlarging the feasible set to earn a lower bound. LPAC does the opposite. It substitutes each block with an explicit expression in $\phi$, $\theta$ and $cs$, so the block is no longer a free variable but a linear formula. Nothing is enlarged, which is why LPAC is an approximation with no bound rather than a relaxation.

Substitution 1: the self block $w_{ii} = v_i^2$

\[w_{ii} = v_i^2 = (1 + \phi_i)^2 = 1 + 2\phi_i + \underbrace{\phi_i^2}_{\text{negligible}} \;\cong\; 1 + 2\phi_i .\]

Substitution 2: the real cross block $w_r = v_i v_j \cos\theta_{ij}$

First, the product of magnitudes:

\[v_i v_j = (1 + \phi_i)(1 + \phi_j) = 1 + \phi_i + \phi_j + \underbrace{\phi_i\phi_j}_{\text{negligible}} \;\cong\; 1 + \phi_i + \phi_j .\]

Now introduce a new variable for the cosine, $cs \cong \cos\theta_{ij}$:

\[w_r \cong (1 + \phi_i + \phi_j)\,cs = cs + (\phi_i + \phi_j)\,cs .\]

Since $cs$ is approximately $1$ in the regime of interest (small angles), write $cs = 1 + (cs - 1)$ with $(cs - 1)$ small. The product $(\phi_i + \phi_j)(cs - 1)$ is small times small (second order) and vanishes:

\[w_r \cong cs + \phi_i + \phi_j .\]

Substitution 3: the imaginary cross block $w_i = v_i v_j \sin\theta_{ij}$

\[w_i \cong \underbrace{(1 + \phi_i + \phi_j)}_{\cong\, 1}\cdot \underbrace{\sin\theta_{ij}}_{\cong\,\theta_{ij}} \;\cong\; \theta_i - \theta_j .\]

Two approximations at once: the magnitude product drops to $1$ (it is not worth keeping the $\phi$ here, because they would multiply $\theta_{ij}$, itself already small, second order again), and the sine becomes the angle, the same $\sin x \cong x$ used by the DC model.

The resulting flows

Substituting the three into the W-space formulas yields the LPAC flows, linear in $\phi$, $\theta$ and $cs$:

\[p_{ij} = \frac{g + g^{fr}}{tm}(1 + 2\phi_i) + \frac{-g\,t_r + b\,t_i}{tm}(cs + \phi_i + \phi_j) + \frac{-b\,t_r - g\,t_i}{tm}\,\theta_{ij}. \tag{1}\]

\[q_{ij} = -\frac{b + b^{fr}}{tm}(1 + 2\phi_i) - \frac{-b\,t_r - g\,t_i}{tm}(cs + \phi_i + \phi_j) + \frac{-g\,t_r + b\,t_i}{tm}\,\theta_{ij}. \tag{2}\]

\[p_{ji} = (g + g^{to})(1 + 2\phi_j) + \frac{-g\,t_r - b\,t_i}{tm}(cs + \phi_i + \phi_j) - \frac{-b\,t_r + g\,t_i}{tm}\,\theta_{ij}. \tag{3}\]

\[q_{ji} = -(b + b^{to})(1 + 2\phi_j) - \frac{-b\,t_r + g\,t_i}{tm}(cs + \phi_i + \phi_j) - \frac{-g\,t_r - b\,t_i}{tm}\,\theta_{ij}, \tag{4}\]

with $\theta_{ij} = \theta_i - \theta_j$. Note what survived in contrast with the DC model: the conductances $g$ are present (so there are losses: $p_{ij} \neq -p_{ji}$), the shunts are present, the complex tap is present, and $q$ exists. The model keeps its AC structure; only the variables have been linearized. The from-side coefficients all carry the $1/tm$ of the complex tap, while the two to-side self terms $(g+g^{to})(1+2\phi_j)$ and $-(b+b^{to})(1+2\phi_j)$ do not, exactly as in the SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation) flows they inherit (the tap sits on the from end).

The cosine variable

The variable $cs$ must behave like $\cos\theta_{ij}$, but the equality $cs = \cos\theta_{ij}$ is non-convex. It is the one nonlinearity the three substitutions could not linearize away, the single trigonometric coupling left in the flows. How that coupling is fenced decides whether the finished model is an LP or a QCQP, as the fidelity note below explains. LPAC fences it two ways.

Box bounds. Exactly the three cases of the QC (Quadratic Convex) model, decided by the position of the angle interval (the cosine is not monotone if the interval crosses zero):

  • If $\theta^{\min} \ge 0$: $\quad \cos\theta^{\max} \le cs \le \cos\theta^{\min}$.
  • If $\theta^{\max} \le 0$: $\quad \cos\theta^{\min} \le cs \le \cos\theta^{\max}$.
  • If it crosses zero: $\quad \min(\cos\theta^{\min}, \cos\theta^{\max}) \le cs \le 1$.

Quadratic cap. And here the parabola of the QC (Quadratic Convex) model reappears:

\[cs \le 1 - \frac{1 - \cos m}{m^2}\,(\theta_i - \theta_j)^2, \qquad m = \max(|\theta^{\min}|, |\theta^{\max}|).\]

Constraints

Voltage limits. Directly on the deviation:

\[V_i^{\min} - 1 \le \phi_i \le V_i^{\max} - 1. \tag{5}\]

Thermal limit. The cone returns, because reactive power is back (in the DC model the limit was a box in $p$):

\[p_{ij}^2 + q_{ij}^2 \le \overline{S}_\ell^2, \qquad p_{ji}^2 + q_{ji}^2 \le \overline{S}_\ell^2. \tag{6}\]

Angular limits.

\[\theta^{\min} \le \theta_i - \theta_j \le \theta^{\max}. \tag{7}\]

LPAC Formulation

The LPAC constructor (lpac(), model type LPAC) assembles the standard skeleton (buses, branches, generators, storage, active/reactive balance, objective) and builds the branch block from the linearized W-space flows derived above. With the cosine held by the parabola and each thermal limit by a second-order cone, the problem is a QCQP, which Lirion solves with Ipopt by default. The flow equations and the voltage, thermal and angle constraints are Eqs. (1)–(7). This section gathers the complete problem around them (sets, parameters, variables, objective, balance, and the remaining blocks) and continues the numbering from (8).

Sets

  • $\mathcal{N}$: set of buses.
  • $\mathcal{N}^{nv} \subseteq \mathcal{N}$: non-virtual buses, on which the power balance is enforced (non_virtual_bus_ids).
  • $\mathcal{L}$: set of active branches (in-service lines and transformers).
  • $\mathcal{G}$: set of existing generators.
  • $\mathcal{G}^c \subseteq \mathcal{G}$: subset of curtailable generators (renewables).
  • $\mathcal{S}$: set of storage units.
  • $\mathcal{T} = \{1,\dots,T\}$: set of time periods in the horizon.

Indices and incidence functions

  • $b, i, j \in \mathcal{N}$: bus indices.
  • $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$: branch index.
  • $g \in \mathcal{G}$: generator index.
  • $s \in \mathcal{S}$: storage index.
  • $t \in \mathcal{T}$: time index.
  • $f(\ell),\ t(\ell)$: from and to buses of branch $\ell$ (code: br.from[l], br.to[l]).
  • $B(s)$: connection bus of storage $s$.

Parameters

Branches.

  • $g_\ell,\ b_\ell$: series conductance and susceptance of branch $\ell$.
  • $g^{fr}_\ell,\ b^{fr}_\ell$: shunt conductance and susceptance, from side.
  • $g^{to}_\ell,\ b^{to}_\ell$: shunt conductance and susceptance, to side.
  • $t_{r,\ell},\ t_{i,\ell}$: real and imaginary parts of the complex tap $T_\ell = \tau_\ell e^{j\Phi_\ell}$ (code: br.tap_real, br.tap_imag).
  • $tm_\ell = |T_\ell|^2 = \tau_\ell^2$: squared tap magnitude; the code multiplies the coefficients below by $1/tm_\ell$ via br.inv_tap[l]^2.
  • $\overline{S}_\ell$: apparent-flow limit of the branch (rate_a).
  • $\theta_\ell^{\min},\ \theta_\ell^{\max}$: angular aperture limits (code: br.ang_min, br.ang_max; NaN means absent).

Buses.

  • $V_b^{\min},\ V_b^{\max}$: voltage magnitude limits at bus $b$.
  • $g^{sh}_b,\ b^{sh}_b$: shunt conductance and susceptance of the bus.
  • $P^d_{b,t},\ Q^d_{b,t}$: active and reactive demand at bus $b$.

Generators.

  • $P_g^{\min},\ P_g^{\max}$: static active-power limits.
  • $\overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t},\ \underline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}$: active-power time envelope.
  • $Q_g^{\min},\ Q_g^{\max}$: static reactive-power limits.
  • $\overline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t},\ \underline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t}$: reactive-power time envelope.
  • $c_{g,t,k}$: coefficients of the cost polynomial of $g$.
  • $c^{curt}_g$: unit curtailment cost of $g$.

Storage. LPAC reuses the DC-style storage block (add_dc_storage_constraints!), so the converter-loss parameters $r_s,x_s,Q^{loss}_s$ of the AC Polar model are not used here.

  • $E_s^{\min},\ E_s^{\max}$: stored-energy limits; $E^0_s$: initial energy.
  • $C_s^{\max},\ D_s^{\max}$: maximum charge and discharge power.
  • $\eta^c_s,\ \eta^d_s$: charge and discharge efficiencies.
  • $P^{loss}_s$: fixed active loss; $\overline{S}_s$: inverter thermal limit.
  • $Q_s^{\min},\ Q_s^{\max}$: reactive-power limits of the storage.

Decision variables

Voltage.

\[\theta_{b,t} \in \mathbb{R} \;\text{: voltage angle at bus } b \text{ at time } t. \tag{8}\]

\[\phi_{b,t} \in [V_b^{\min}-1,\ V_b^{\max}-1] \;\text{: voltage-magnitude deviation} \;(v_b = 1 + \phi_b),\quad \text{see Eq. (5)}.\]

Cosine surrogate. One $cs_{\ell,t}$ per branch, box-bounded by the angle regime (exactly the three QC (Quadratic Convex) cases; a fourth, default branch applies when the angle limits are absent):

\[cs_{\ell,t} \in \begin{cases} [\cos\theta_\ell^{\max},\ \cos\theta_\ell^{\min}], & \theta_\ell^{\min}\ge 0,\\[2pt] [\cos\theta_\ell^{\min},\ \cos\theta_\ell^{\max}], & \theta_\ell^{\max}\le 0,\\[2pt] [\min(\cos\theta_\ell^{\min},\cos\theta_\ell^{\max}),\ 1], & \theta_\ell^{\min}<0<\theta_\ell^{\max},\\[2pt] [-1,\ 1], & \theta_\ell^{\min},\theta_\ell^{\max}\ \text{absent (NaN)}. \end{cases} \tag{9}\]

Generation.

\[p^g_{g,t} \in [P_g^{\min}, P_g^{\max}] \;\text{: active power generated by } g. \tag{10}\]

\[Q^g_{g,t} \in [Q_g^{\min}, Q_g^{\max}] \;\text{: reactive power generated by } g. \tag{11}\]

\[p^c_{g,t} \in [0, \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}] \;\text{: curtailed renewable power, } g \in \mathcal{G}^c. \tag{12}\]

Storage (only when $\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset$): $E_{s,t}\in[E_s^{\min},E_s^{\max}]$, $c_{s,t}\in[0,C_s^{\max}]$, $d_{s,t}\in[0,D_s^{\max}]$, $P^s_{s,t}\in[-\overline{S}_s,\overline{S}_s]$, $Q^s_{s,t}\in[Q_s^{\min},Q_s^{\max}]$, and $Q^{sc}_{s,t}\in[Q_s^{\min},Q_s^{\max}]$ (the converter-compensation variable qsc is created but is inert under the DC-style storage block, as noted below).

Objective function

Identical to the AC Polar model: fuel cost on the power in physical units ($S^{base}\,p^g$) plus renewable curtailment cost.

\[F = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}} \sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}} \sum_{k=1}^{m_{g,t}} c_{g,t,k}\left(S^{base}\,p^g_{g,t}\right)^{m_{g,t} - k}. \tag{13}\]

\[F^{curt} = \sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}^c} \sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}} c^{curt}_g\,S^{base}\,p^c_{g,t}, \qquad \min\; F + F^{curt}. \tag{14}\]

Branch flows

The four linearized W-space flows $P^f_{\ell,t}, Q^f_{\ell,t}, P^t_{\ell,t}, Q^t_{\ell,t}$ are Eqs. (1)–(4), linear in $\phi$, $cs$ and $\theta$, with $\theta_{ij} = \theta_{f(\ell)} - \theta_{t(\ell)}$ and all from-side coefficients scaled by $1/tm_\ell$. The implementation reifies these four flows as explicit variables bounded to $[-\overline{S}_\ell, \overline{S}_\ell]$, each tied by equality to its expression, so the thermal cone (6) is a second-order cone in two named variables per side. Those box bounds are already implied by (6); they condition the solver and add no law to the model.

Power balance

Active power. The bus shunt enters as the linearized $g^{sh}_b(1 + 2\phi_b)$:

\[\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}(b)} p^g_{g,t} - \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}(b)} P^s_{s,t} - P^d_{b,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,f(\ell)=b} P^f_{\ell,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,t(\ell)=b} P^t_{\ell,t} - g^{sh}_b\,(1 + 2\phi_{b,t}) = 0, \qquad b\in\mathcal{N}^{nv},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{15}\]

Reactive power. The bus shunt enters as $+\,b^{sh}_b(1 + 2\phi_b)$:

\[\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}(b)} Q^g_{g,t} - \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}(b)} Q^s_{s,t} - Q^d_{b,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,f(\ell)=b} Q^f_{\ell,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,t(\ell)=b} Q^t_{\ell,t} + b^{sh}_b\,(1 + 2\phi_{b,t}) = 0, \qquad b\in\mathcal{N}^{nv},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{16}\]

Cosine envelope

Besides the box (9), $cs$ is capped by the QC (Quadratic Convex) parabola, added only when $m_\ell = \max(|\theta_\ell^{\min}|,|\theta_\ell^{\max}|) > 0$:

\[cs_{\ell,t} \le 1 - \frac{1 - \cos m_\ell}{m_\ell^2}\, \big(\theta_{f(\ell),t} - \theta_{t(\ell),t}\big)^2, \qquad \ell\in\mathcal{L},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{17}\]

Branch thermal and angle limits

The second-order cone (6) on each side, and the angle-aperture limits (7), each bound imposed only when the corresponding $\theta_\ell^{\min}/\theta_\ell^{\max}$ is present (not NaN).

Reference bus

\[\theta_{ref,t} = 0, \qquad t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{18}\]

Generation constraints

Time envelopes (added only when strictly tighter than the static bounds) and the renewable curtailment identity. The per-period upper active bound is skipped for renewables, which take the curtailment identity instead; the lower active bound and the reactive envelope apply to every generator:

\[p^g_{g,t} \le \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}\ (g\notin\mathcal{G}^c), \qquad \underline{P}^{ts}_{g,t} \le p^g_{g,t}\ (g\in\mathcal{G}), \qquad \underline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t} \le Q^g_{g,t} \le \overline{Q}^{ts}_{g,t}, \qquad t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{19}\]

\[p^g_{g,t} + p^c_{g,t} = \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}, \qquad g\in\mathcal{G}^c,\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{20}\]

Storage constraints (DC-style)

When $\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset$ the shared DC storage block is added. It differs from the AC Polar storage in that the active injection carries only the fixed loss $P^{loss}_s$ (no $r_s|S|^2/V^2$ converter term) and there is no reactive-coupling equation (the qsc variable is created but unused):

\[E_{s,1} = E^0_s + \eta^c_s\,c_{s,1} - \frac{d_{s,1}}{\eta^d_s}, \qquad E_{s,t} = E_{s,t-1} + \eta^c_s\,c_{s,t} - \frac{d_{s,t}}{\eta^d_s}\ (t\ge 2). \tag{21}\]

\[c_{s,t}\,d_{s,t} = 0, \qquad (P^s_{s,t})^2 + (Q^s_{s,t})^2 \le \overline{S}_s^2, \qquad P^s_{s,t} + d_{s,t} - c_{s,t} = P^{loss}_s. \tag{22}\]

Complete model

\[\min_{\substack{\theta,\,\phi,\,cs,\,p^g,\,Q^g,\,p^c,\\ E,\,c,\,d,\,P^s,\,Q^s,\,Q^{sc}}} \quad F + F^{curt} \qquad (\text{Eq. } 14)\]

subject to:

What it captures / what it loses

Philosophy

Captures: voltage magnitude (through the deviation $\phi$), reactive power $q$, ohmic losses ($p_{ij} \neq -p_{ji}$, since the conductances $g$ survive), line shunts, and the complex transformer tap. In form the model is still AC. Loses: the exact cosine (replaced by the $cs$ box and parabolic cap) and all second-order terms ($\phi^2$, $\phi_i\phi_j$, $(\phi_i+\phi_j)(cs-1)$, and the $(\phi_i+\phi_j)\theta_{ij}$ of the imaginary block). Being an approximation, its optimum is neither a floor nor a ceiling on the true AC cost. But it errs far less than the DC model, and over a far wider set of regimes.

When to trust it

LPAC is at its best where its linearization holds and no bound is required: a transmission network near nominal voltage, where $\phi$ stays small enough that $\phi^2$ is genuinely negligible, and angle differences stay modest so the cosine surrogate and $\sin\theta \cong \theta$ both stay accurate. There it recovers what the DC model cannot see, the voltage magnitude, reactive power and losses, while staying convex and cheap to solve. Prefer it over DC whenever reactive support, voltage limits, or losses are part of the question, or whenever the network is loaded past the near-nominal regime the DC model assumes.

It stops being reliable when $\phi$ grows large, under heavy loading or voltage stress, where $\phi^2$ is no longer negligible, and when angle differences widen past the range where the cosine surrogate is tight. When the answer must be a certified bound rather than an estimate, use a relaxation instead (SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation), QC (Quadratic Convex), SDPWRM). When it must be exact, the AC Polar model.

Lirion vs. the original

Fidelity note

The original LPAC article of Coffrin and Van Hentenryck (2014) fences the cosine with a piecewise-linear approximation, a bundle of tangent lines, $cs \le \cos\hat\theta - \sin\hat\theta\,(\theta_{ij} - \hat\theta)$, one for each chosen angle point $\hat\theta$. This keeps the model strictly LP, which is the reason for the name. Lirion instead implements the cosine with the parabola, more compact (one constraint instead of a bundle) and tighter near the centre, at the cost of leaving LP: with the parabola the model is a QCQP. The same divergence appears in the thermal limit. Lirion keeps the second-order cone. The original article writes the apparent-power limit as the same quadratic circle, an optional added constraint, and only remarks that it can be replaced by piecewise-linear cuts (a polygon) to stay inside LP. It does not build that polygon itself.

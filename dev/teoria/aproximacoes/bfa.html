BFA (LinDistFlow)

The branch-flow approximation (BFA) is the last rung of the branch-flow family. It stands to the branch-flow second-order-cone model (SOCBF) much as the DC model stands to the AC model: a linearization of the model one level up. The step is narrow. One quantity leaves, the squared branch current, and with it the only nonlinearity in the branch physics. In the distribution literature the model is known as LinDistFlow [5], and it is probably the most widely used linear network model in distribution optimization, for Volt/Var control, distributed-generation siting, and feeder reconfiguration. See [6] for the distribution-system modelling context.

Approximation, not relaxation

Like the DC and LPAC models, the BFA is an approximation, not a relaxation: its optimum is neither a floor nor a ceiling of the true cost.

Derivation

The branch-flow starting point

The branch-flow view describes each branch $\ell$ by the flows $p_{ij}, q_{ij}$ that enter it at its from bus $i$ and the flows $p_{ji}, q_{ji}$ that enter at its to bus $j$, together with the squared magnitude of the current it carries, $|I_\ell|^2$. Each bus holds a single voltage state, the squared magnitude $w_b = |V_b|^2$. Two exact relations tie a branch to its two end buses: the power it loses, and the drop in squared voltage from one end to the other. On a branch with no shunt admittance, where the algebra stays readable, they are

\[p_{ij} + p_{ji} = r_\ell\,|I_\ell|^2, \qquad \frac{w_i}{tm_\ell} - w_j = 2\,(r_\ell\,p_{ij} + x_\ell\,q_{ij}) - (r_\ell^2 + x_\ell^2)\,|I_\ell|^2,\]

with $tm_\ell$ the squared tap magnitude. The current magnitude has its own defining relation, $|I_\ell|^2 = (p_{ij}^2 + q_{ij}^2)\big/(w_i/tm_\ell)$, which the SOCBF model carries as a rotated second-order cone. That cone is the one nonlinearity among the branch relations, and it is what the approximation targets. It is a different object from the apparent-power thermal limit the formulation keeps, which stays a cone further down.

One term fewer

With the branch-flow relations in hand, the approximation is a single one:

\[|I_\ell|^2 \cong 0,\]

the squared branch current neglected wherever it appears. This does not mean the branch carries no current. The flows stay; the only things dropped are the small terms built from $|I_\ell|^2$, and with them the defining relation for $|I_\ell|^2$ itself. The rest of the derivation is a matter of seeing what survives in each equation.

What justifies the discard

Compare the two terms of the voltage-drop equation:

\[\underbrace{2\,(r\,p_{ij} + x\,q_{ij})}_{\text{principal term}} \quad\text{versus}\quad \underbrace{(r^2 + x^2)\,|I_\ell|^2}_{\text{discarded term}}.\]

The first is linear in the impedance, the second quadratic ($r^2+x^2$). In per unit, branch impedances are small numbers, a typical feeder having $r$ in the range $0.01$ to $0.1$, so the square is an order of magnitude smaller. It is the same logic as the LPAC model discarding $\phi^2$: keep the first order, throw away the second.

Quantifying the error

The neglected $|I_\ell|^2$ sits in the branch losses (the resistive $r_\ell\,|I_\ell|^2$ and the reactive $x_\ell\,|I_\ell|^2$) and in the quadratic $(r_\ell^2+x_\ell^2)\,|I_\ell|^2$ of the voltage drop. With $|I_\ell|^2 \cong (p^2+q^2)/w$ ($w$ the squared voltage magnitude) and the voltage near $1$, these sit at order $r\cdot p$ in per unit relative to the terms they correct, for a line with $r\cong x$ and $p\cong q$. The error therefore grows with the resistance and with the loading:

  • on a line with $r=0.05$ and a flow of $0.3$ pu, the neglected part is $\cong 1.5\%$ of the drop;
  • at a flow of $1.0$ pu it is $\cong 5\%$.

In the load-dominated regime these feeders normally run, the bias has a definite sign. Dropping the branch losses lets each line carry only its net load, without the extra current the losses really draw, so its flows run short of the true ones and the computed drop comes out low: the BFA underestimates the voltage drop, and its voltages sit a little high. The quadratic term in the drop, which is subtracted there, would on its own lengthen the computed drop and so pulls the other way, but it offsets only half of the loss effect and cannot reverse the sign. Under heavy distributed generation, where power flows back up the feeder, the sign can turn over with it. This load-dominated bias is the familiar behaviour of LinDistFlow.

The surviving branch relations

Besides the current-magnitude definition the starting point gave, the SOCBF ties each branch to its end buses through the power losses and the voltage drop. Setting $|I_\ell|^2 \cong 0$ removes the current-magnitude definition outright (there is nothing left to define) and trims the other two. Restoring the line charging the starting point set aside, here is what the losses and the drop keep.

Losses. The active- and reactive-power sums across the branch keep only their shunt terms, the series losses going with $|I_\ell|^2$:

\[p_{ij} + p_{ji} = r\,|I_\ell|^2 + \frac{g^{fr}}{tm}\,w_i + g^{to}\,w_j \;\longrightarrow\; p_{ij} + p_{ji} = \frac{g^{fr}}{tm}\,w_i + g^{to}\,w_j,\]

\[q_{ij} + q_{ji} = x\,|I_\ell|^2 - \frac{b^{fr}}{tm}\,w_i - b^{to}\,w_j \;\longrightarrow\; q_{ij} + q_{ji} = -\frac{b^{fr}}{tm}\,w_i - b^{to}\,w_j.\]

Here $w_i$ and $w_j$ are the squared voltage magnitudes $|V_i|^2$ and $|V_j|^2$ at the from and to buses (the LinDistFlow voltage state, one value per bus), and $tm = |T|^2$ is the squared tap magnitude, as on the AC Polar and LPAC pages. The series losses $r\,|I_\ell|^2$ and $x\,|I_\ell|^2$ become zero, but the shunt losses stay. This contrasts with the DC model. There $p_{ij} = -p_{ji}$ exactly, the model was rigorously lossless, whereas here $p_{ij} + p_{ji} \neq 0$ in general, because the shunt still drains power, a drain proportional to $|V|^2$. In practice that drain is small: the shunt conductance $g^{fr}, g^{to}$ is usually near zero (line charging is almost purely reactive), so the kept loss is tiny and the BFA runs close to lossless, which is part of why its voltages come out high.

Voltage drop. Setting $|I_\ell|^2 \cong 0$ removes the quadratic $(r^2+x^2)\,|I_\ell|^2$ from the Ohm's-law relation. With the line charging back in, the exact relation also carries a coefficient $1 + 2(r\,g^{fr} - x\,b^{fr})$ on $w_i/tm$; the BFA rounds it to $1$. This is a further, smaller simplification, built from the charging shunt rather than the current, and it is identically zero on a branch without charging, which is why the shunt-free derivation above saw only the single discard. What is left is a linear tie between the two bus voltages:

\[\frac{w_i}{tm} - w_j = 2\,(r\,p_{ij} + x\,q_{ij}),\]

the classical LinDistFlow drop: the squared magnitude falls by twice the real part of the impedance times the flow.

BFA Formulation

The BFA constructor (bfa(), model type BFA) assembles the standard skeleton (buses, branches, generators, storage, active/reactive balance, objective) in W-space: the only voltage variable is the squared magnitude $w_b = |V_b|^2$ per bus. This is the per-bus voltage state the W-space relaxations also use, but the BFA ties buses together through the linear voltage drop (8) alone, not through the off-diagonal bus-pair products and Jabr cone of SOCWR (Jabr conic relaxation). The branch relations above are linear, but the thermal limit is kept as a second-order cone (see the fidelity note below), so the problem is an SOCP, which Lirion solves with Clarabel by default. The cone raises the solver class, not the model's standing: it is an exact operating limit, not a relaxed physical law, so the BFA stays an approximation and its optimum is still no bound.

Sets

  • $\mathcal{N}$: set of buses.
  • $\mathcal{N}^{nv} \subseteq \mathcal{N}$: non-virtual buses, on which the power balance is enforced (non_virtual_bus_ids).
  • $\mathcal{L}$: set of active branches.
  • $\mathcal{G}$: generators; $\mathcal{G}^c \subseteq \mathcal{G}$: curtailable (renewable) generators.
  • $\mathcal{S}$: storage units.
  • $\mathcal{T} = \{1,\dots,T\}$: time periods.

Indices and incidence functions

  • $b, i, j \in \mathcal{N}$; $\ell \in \mathcal{L}$; $g \in \mathcal{G}$; $s \in \mathcal{S}$; $t \in \mathcal{T}$.
  • $f(\ell),\ t(\ell)$: from and to buses of branch $\ell$ (code: br.from[l], br.to[l]).
  • $B(s)$: connection bus of storage $s$.

Parameters

  • $r_\ell,\ x_\ell$: series resistance and reactance of branch $\ell$.
  • $g^{fr}_\ell,\ b^{fr}_\ell$; $g^{to}_\ell,\ b^{to}_\ell$: shunt conductance and susceptance of the from and to ends.
  • $tm_\ell = |T_\ell|^2 = \tau_\ell^2$: squared tap magnitude; the code writes the tap denominator as $\texttt{inv\_tap}^2 = 1/tm_\ell$.
  • $\overline{S}_\ell$: apparent-power (thermal) limit of the branch (rate_a).
  • $\theta^{\min}_\ell,\ \theta^{\max}_\ell$: angular-aperture limits (ang_min, ang_max; NaN means absent).
  • $V_b^{\min},\ V_b^{\max}$: voltage-magnitude limits; $g^{sh}_b,\ b^{sh}_b$: bus shunt conductance and susceptance; $P^d_{b,t},\ Q^d_{b,t}$: active and reactive demand.
  • Generation and DC-style storage parameters exactly as in the LPAC model.

Decision variables

\[w_{b,t} \in [(V_b^{\min})^2,\ (V_b^{\max})^2] \;\text{: squared voltage magnitude } |V_b|^2\ (\texttt{v\_magnitude\_sq}). \tag{1}\]

\[P^f_{\ell,t},\ Q^f_{\ell,t},\ P^t_{\ell,t},\ Q^t_{\ell,t} \in [-\overline{S}_\ell,\ \overline{S}_\ell] \;\text{: from- and to-end active and reactive flows.} \tag{2}\]

\[p^g_{g,t} \in [P_g^{\min}, P_g^{\max}],\quad Q^g_{g,t} \in [Q_g^{\min}, Q_g^{\max}],\quad p^c_{g,t} \in [0, \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}]\,(g\in\mathcal{G}^c) \;\text{: generation and curtailment.} \tag{3}\]

Storage, when $\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset$, adds the DC-style block $E_{s,t}, c_{s,t}, d_{s,t}, P^s_{s,t}, Q^s_{s,t}, Q^{sc}_{s,t}$ of the LPAC model. There is no squared-current variable: the $|I_\ell|^2$ of SOCBF is exactly what the approximation removed.

Objective function

Identical to the AC Polar model: fuel cost on power in physical units plus renewable curtailment cost.

\[F = \sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}\sum_{k=1}^{m_{g,t}} c_{g,t,k}\,(S^{base}\,p^g_{g,t})^{m_{g,t}-k},\qquad F^{curt} = \sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}^c}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}} c^{curt}_g\,S^{base}\,p^c_{g,t}. \tag{4}\]

\[\min\; F + F^{curt}. \tag{5}\]

Branch relations

The losses carry only the shunt terms, and the voltage drop is linear in the flows (the two relations derived above), per branch $\ell$ and time $t$:

\[P^f_{\ell,t} + P^t_{\ell,t} = \frac{g^{fr}_\ell}{tm_\ell}\,w_{f(\ell),t} + g^{to}_\ell\,w_{t(\ell),t}, \tag{6}\]

\[Q^f_{\ell,t} + Q^t_{\ell,t} = -\frac{b^{fr}_\ell}{tm_\ell}\,w_{f(\ell),t} - b^{to}_\ell\,w_{t(\ell),t}, \tag{7}\]

\[\frac{w_{f(\ell),t}}{tm_\ell} - w_{t(\ell),t} = 2\,(r_\ell\,P^f_{\ell,t} + x_\ell\,Q^f_{\ell,t}). \tag{8}\]

Branch thermal limits

A second-order cone on each end (not a box), the one nonlinearity the model keeps. It is an operating limit, not a branch relation, and a different cone from the current-magnitude one the approximation dropped:

\[(P^f_{\ell,t})^2 + (Q^f_{\ell,t})^2 \le \overline{S}_\ell^2,\qquad (P^t_{\ell,t})^2 + (Q^t_{\ell,t})^2 \le \overline{S}_\ell^2. \tag{9}\]

Angle limits

The angular aperture is enforced in branch-flow form, as a pair of linear cuts on the flows and the from-bus voltage (reused from SOCBF, _add_socbf_angle_constraints!), posted per branch only where $\theta^{\min}_\ell$ or $\theta^{\max}_\ell$ is set:

\[\tan(\theta^{\min}_\ell)\,\mathrm{Re}_{\ell,t} \le \mathrm{Im}_{\ell,t} \le \tan(\theta^{\max}_\ell)\,\mathrm{Re}_{\ell,t}, \tag{10}\]

where $\mathrm{Re}_{\ell,t}$ and $\mathrm{Im}_{\ell,t}$ are the real and imaginary parts of a branch-flow angle surrogate, each linear in $w_{f(\ell),t}$, $P^f_{\ell,t}$ and $Q^f_{\ell,t}$. Their ratio $\mathrm{Im}/\mathrm{Re}$ stands in for the tangent of the angle across the branch, so holding it between $\tan\theta^{\min}$ and $\tan\theta^{\max}$ bounds the aperture. With no angle variable in W-space, these two linear combinations are what the model carries in place of $\theta_{f(\ell)} - \theta_{t(\ell)}$.

Power balance

At each non-virtual bus the shunt rides on the squared magnitude $w_{b,t}$ (not a constant, as in the DC and NFA models, because here the voltage is a variable):

\[\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}(b)} p^g_{g,t} - \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}(b)} P^s_{s,t} - P^d_{b,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,f(\ell)=b} P^f_{\ell,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,t(\ell)=b} P^t_{\ell,t} - g^{sh}_b\,w_{b,t} = 0, \qquad b\in\mathcal{N}^{nv},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{11}\]

\[\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}(b)} Q^g_{g,t} - \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}(b)} Q^s_{s,t} - Q^d_{b,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,f(\ell)=b} Q^f_{\ell,t} - \sum_{\ell:\,t(\ell)=b} Q^t_{\ell,t} + b^{sh}_b\,w_{b,t} = 0, \qquad b\in\mathcal{N}^{nv},\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{12}\]

There is no reference-voltage equation. The magnitudes are held by their bounds (1) and tied to one another by the voltage drop (8), and with no angle variable anywhere in the model there is no angle reference to set.

Generation and storage

The active and reactive generation envelopes (applied as the tighter of the static and per-period bounds) and the renewable curtailment identity are those of the LPAC model:

\[p^g_{g,t} + p^c_{g,t} = \overline{P}^{ts}_{g,t}, \qquad g\in\mathcal{G}^c,\ t\in\mathcal{T}. \tag{13}\]

Storage, when present, is the shared DC-style block (add_dc_storage_constraints!): energy balance, a binary-free charge/discharge exclusivity, and the converter limit, exactly as in the DC and LPAC models. The reactive storage variables are created but stay decoupled from the DC-style block.

What it captures / what it loses

Philosophy

Captures: the voltage-magnitude profile through the linear drop (8), reactive power, the shunt losses, and the apparent-power thermal limit as an exact cone. On a radial feeder near nominal voltage the result is close to the full AC solution.

Loses: the resistive (Joule) loss $r\,|I_\ell|^2$ and the quadratic voltage-drop term $(r^2+x^2)\,|I_\ell|^2$, both dropped with $|I_\ell|^2$. With the losses gone the branch flows carry only the net load, so the model underestimates the voltage drop and runs the voltages a little high; the error grows with resistance and loading. Being an approximation, its optimum is neither a floor nor a ceiling on the true AC cost.

When to trust it

The BFA works best on radial or lightly meshed distribution feeders near nominal voltage, where the squared current is small. That is the regime LinDistFlow was built for. It stops being reliable as resistance and loading rise, where the dropped $|I_\ell|^2$ terms grow and the voltage drop is increasingly underestimated. When a certified bound is needed rather than an estimate, use the SOCBF relaxation it linearizes; for the exact answer, the AC Polar model.

Lirion vs. the original

Fidelity note

The textbook LinDistFlow of Baran and Wu [5] is a linear model, written for radial feeders and dropping the branch charging. It carries no apparent-power term of its own, so linear-programming implementations either leave the thermal limit out or bound the apparent power with a linear box or polygon. Lirion keeps the two features that make its branch-flow family uniform, so its BFA is slightly richer than the classical model and is not a pure LP:

  • the thermal limit stays the exact apparent-power cone (9) rather than a box or polygon, the same apparent-power limit PowerModels' BFAPowerModel enforces (PowerModels writes it as a convex quadratic, Lirion as the equivalent cone), which makes the model an SOCP solved with Clarabel;
  • the shunt terms $g^{fr}, b^{fr}, g^{to}, b^{to}$ and the complex tap are kept, so the losses (6), (7) and the drop (8) carry the tap and shunt data a bare radial model omits.

The $\pm\overline{S}_\ell$ bounds on the flow variables (2) are left in place but are redundant once the cone (9) is posted.
